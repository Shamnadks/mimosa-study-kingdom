4.1 National Health Intervention Programme for Mother and Child

Book section 4.1 · RCH → NRHM → RMNCH+A — a whole “continuum of care” from adolescence to childhood.

🏹 Story mode — Brave

In Brave, Queen Elinor turns into a bear and Merida has to mend the bond between them before it's too late. The film's real lesson: mother and child are one unit — when one is in danger, so is the other. India's mother-and-child programmes work on that idea. Instead of one scheme for pregnancy and another for babies, they follow the girl from adolescence → marriage and family planning → pregnancy and birth → the newborn → childhood → and back to the next adolescent. That circle is called the continuum of care, and it is the heart of RMNCH+A.

📘 Exam definition (write this)

The national health intervention programme for mother and child is the package of Government of India programmes, now delivered under the RMNCH+A strategy (2013) of the National Health Mission, that provides a continuum of preventive, promotive and curative care across the life stages of women and children in order to reduce maternal, neonatal, infant and child mortality and morbidity.

Psst, Mimosa… NMR is ~70% of IMR in India (19 of 27) — newborn care is where lives are still being lost. That's why HBNC and SNCUs exist! 👶

Evolution — from family planning to RMNCH+A

RMNCH+A — the 2013 strategy (5 pillars)

RMNCH+A = Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child health + Adolescent health. It was launched in February 2013 under NHM. Its two big ideas: (1) the life-cycle approach — care at every stage, not only at delivery; (2) the continuum of care across levels — home/community → sub-centre → PHC → CHC/FRU → district hospital.

Objectives (write 5–6)

  1. Reduce MMR, NMR, IMR and U5MR to national / SDG targets.
  2. Promote institutional delivery and skilled birth attendance.
  3. Ensure full antenatal care (registration in 1st trimester, ≥4 ANC visits, IFA, Td, calcium, deworming) and postnatal care.
  4. Achieve full immunisation of all children and pregnant women.
  5. Prevent and manage childhood illness (diarrhoea, pneumonia), malnutrition and anaemia.
  6. Provide family-planning services for spacing and limiting births.
  7. Address adolescent nutrition, reproductive and mental health.

🆕 Update note

NFHS-6 (2023–24) was released in May 2026: institutional deliveries 90.6%, ANC coverage 95.9%, stunting in under-5s fell to 29.3% (from 35.5%). Quote one NFHS-6 number — it shows the examiner your notes are current.

💊 Pharmacist's role
  • Dispense & counsel on IFA (take after meals, black stools are harmless), calcium (not with IFA), albendazole, ORS–zinc.
  • Cold-chain care of vaccines in the PHC store; check VVM before issue.
  • Part of the RBSK mobile health team; manages drug logistics for JSSK “free drugs”.
  • Counsel on drugs to avoid in pregnancy & lactation; promote exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months.
⚠️ Classic confusion

JSY ≠ JSSK. JSY (2005) pays cash to the mother for delivering in hospital; JSSK (2011) makes everything free inside the hospital. Also: Maternal Mortality Ratio is per 1,00,000 live births — don't write “per 1000”.

4.2 National Family Welfare Programme

Book section 4.2 · India was the first country in the world to start a national family planning programme (1952).

🏠 Story mode — Encanto

The Madrigals live in a magical Casita, and Abuela Alma wants every member to be strong and happy. But the house cracks when the family is stretched too thin and nobody's needs are heard. Family welfare is India's way of keeping the Casita strong: couples freely choose how many children and when, mothers stay healthy between births, and every child gets enough care. Notice the key word — freely. Since 1977 the programme is voluntary; since 1996 it has no targets.

📘 Exam definitions

Family planning (WHO Expert Committee, 1971): a way of thinking and living that is adopted voluntarily, on the basis of knowledge, attitudes and responsible decisions by individuals and couples, to promote the health and welfare of the family group and so contribute to the social development of the country.

Family welfare is the wider concept: family planning plus maternal and child health, nutrition, immunisation and health education — the welfare of the whole family, not just birth control.

Objectives of the National Family Welfare Programme

  1. Reduce the birth rate and attain the replacement-level TFR of 2.1, leading to population stabilisation.
  2. Promote the small family norm through voluntary, informed choice.
  3. Provide a wide basket of contraceptive choices — spacing and limiting methods — free of cost.
  4. Promote spacing between births (ideally ≥ 3 years) for the health of mother and child.
  5. Reduce MMR and IMR by integrating family planning with MCH services.
  6. Meet the unmet need for contraception; delay age at marriage and first pregnancy.

National Population Policy 2000 — three objectives

⏱️ImmediateMeet unmet needs for contraception, health infrastructure & personnel; integrated RCH service delivery
📉Medium-termBring TFR to replacement level by 2010
⚖️Long-termStable population by 2045, consistent with sustainable development

NPP 2000 listed 14 National Socio-Demographic Goals for 2010, for example: IMR below 30/1000, MMR below 100/1,00,000, universal immunisation, 80% institutional deliveries and 100% deliveries by trained persons, marriage of girls not before 18 (preferably after 20), free compulsory schooling to age 14, 100% registration of births, deaths, marriages and pregnancies, and promotion of the small family norm.

Methods offered — the “basket of choice”

Services & schemes under the programme

🚚 Delivery of services

  • Free contraceptives at all public facilities; home delivery of contraceptives by ASHA.
  • Ensuring Spacing at Birth — ASHA counselling of newly-weds and after first child.
  • Post-partum family planning: PPIUCD, post-partum sterilisation.
  • Fixed-day static services; sterilisation camps meeting quality standards.

💰 Support & safety

  • Compensation scheme for sterilisation acceptors (wage loss).
  • Family Planning Indemnity Scheme — cover for failure/complication/death after sterilisation.
  • FP-LMIS — logistics software to avoid stock-outs.
  • World Population Day fortnight (11 July) and Vasectomy fortnight for awareness.

India has therefore met the NPP-2000 medium-term goal (replacement-level fertility), though later than 2010, and the population is now heading towards stabilisation. Challenges: dependence on female sterilisation, low male participation, regional gaps (Bihar, UP, Meghalaya above replacement), and the need for more spacing methods.

💊 Pharmacist's role
  • Counsel OCP users: start day, daily same time, missed-pill rule, drug interactions (rifampicin, some antiepileptics reduce efficacy).
  • Dispense emergency contraception responsibly — “emergency, not regular”.
  • Explain Chhaya's weekly schedule (twice a week for the first 3 months, then once a week).
  • Promote condoms for dual protection; bust myths about IUCDs and NSV; maintain stocks; report ADRs.
⚠️ Common mistake

“Family planning” = only birth control; “family welfare” = the whole package. And remember: the programme is target-free since 1996 — don't write that India still fixes sterilisation targets.

4.3 National Tobacco Control Programme (NTCP)

Book section 4.3 “National tobacco control in India” · NTCP launched 2007–08 to put COTPA 2003 and WHO FCTC into action.

🚬 Story mode — 101 Dalmatians

Cruella de Vil is never seen without her long cigarette holder, puffing green smoke over everyone around her — the puppies, Roger, Anita, even her own henchmen. That's tobacco in one picture: it harms the smoker and the bystanders (passive smoking). NTCP is the plan to take that holder away: ban smoking in public places, stop the glamour ads, keep it away from children, scare people with pack warnings, and help Cruella quit.

The burden — why India needs NTCP

📊

28.6% adults

use tobacco (GATS-2, 2016–17) ≈ 26.7 crore people — men 42.4%, women 14.2%. Down from 34.6% in GATS-1 (2009–10).
⚰️

≈ 13.5 lakh deaths/yr

are attributed to tobacco in India (MoHFW estimate).
🍃

Smokeless dominates

Khaini, gutkha, zarda, paan with tobacco, mishri, gul — besides bidi, cigarette, hookah, chutta.

NTCP — Launch, nodal body & objectives

Launched: 2007–08, during the 11th Five-Year Plan, initially in 42 districts of 21 States/UTs; now in all States/UTs. Nodal body: National Tobacco Control Cell (NTCC), MoHFW. Since the 12th Plan, state & district components are funded through the NCD flexi-pool of NHM.

📘 Objectives of NTCP (2-mark favourite — write all five)
  1. Create awareness about the harmful effects of tobacco consumption.
  2. Reduce the production and supply of tobacco products.
  3. Ensure effective implementation of COTPA 2003.
  4. Help people quit tobacco use (cessation).
  5. Facilitate implementation of strategies for prevention and control of tobacco advocated by WHO FCTC.

💊 Pharmacist's role — the 5 A's & 5 R's

5 A's (willing to quit): Ask about use · Advise to quit · Assess readiness · Assist (quit plan + medicines) · Arrange follow-up.
5 R's (not yet willing): Relevance · Risks · Rewards · Roadblocks · Repetition.
Medicines: nicotine replacement therapy (gum, patch, lozenge), bupropion, varenicline. Pharmacies themselves must not sell tobacco.

⚠️ Don't mix up

COTPA is the 2003 law; NTCP is the 2007–08 programme that implements it; FCTC is the WHO treaty. Write all three, in that order, in any tobacco answer.

4.4 National Malaria Prevention Programme

Book section 4.4 · NMCP 1953 → NMEP 1958 → NAMP 1999 → NVBDCP 2003 → National Framework for Malaria Elimination 2016–2030.

🐸 Story mode — The Princess and the Frog

Down in the Louisiana bayou, Tiana and Naveen (as frogs) are chased by clouds of hungry mosquitoes — while Ray the firefly lights the way and Mama Odie dispenses wisdom. Malaria control is exactly that bayou battle: drain and treat the swampy breeding places, keep the biting mosquitoes away at night (nets, sprays), find every fever fast (Ray's light = rapid diagnosis!), cure it completely (Mama Odie's medicine = ACT/chloroquine + primaquine) — until not a single local case is left.

📘 Exam definition

The National Malaria Prevention (Control/Elimination) Programme is the Government of India programme, implemented under the National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP) of NHM, that aims to reduce malaria illness and deaths and to eliminate malaria (zero indigenous cases) from India by 2030, through early diagnosis and complete treatment, integrated vector management and strong surveillance.

Quick recap — the disease

🦠

Agent

Plasmodium vivax & P. falciparum (most cases in India); also P. malariae, P. ovale. Pf causes severe/cerebral malaria; Pv relapses from liver hypnozoites.
🦟

Vector

Female Anopheles (night biter). Rural: An. culicifacies; urban: An. stephensi; hilly/foothills: An. fluviatilis; North-East: An. minimus, An. baimaii; coastal: An. sundaicus.
🌡️

Clinical

Fever with chills & rigors, sweating, headache, anaemia, splenomegaly; Pf can cause coma, renal failure, death.

🆕 Update note

The NVBDCP directorate is now called the National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control (NCVBDC) (renamed around 2022); it still runs the NVBDCP. In the exam, write “NVBDCP (now NCVBDC)”.

NFME 2016–2030 — goals, objectives & categories

Goals: (1) eliminate malaria (zero indigenous cases) throughout India by 2030; (2) maintain malaria-free status and prevent re-establishment.

📘 NFME objectives (with years)
  1. Eliminate malaria from all 26 low (Category 1) and moderate (Category 2) transmission States/UTs by 2022.
  2. Reduce incidence to < 1 case per 1000 population per year in all States/UTs and their districts by 2024.
  3. Interrupt indigenous transmission in all States/UTs, including high-transmission (Category 3) ones, by 2027.
  4. Prevent re-establishment of local transmission and maintain national malaria-free status by 2030 and beyond.

Strategies / functioning (write as headings)

1 · Case management (EDCT)

  • Early diagnosis — bivalent RDT by ASHA/MPW, microscopy at PHC (“test every fever”).
  • Complete treatment (see table) within 24 h of diagnosis; radical cure with primaquine.
  • Severe malaria → injectable artesunate at referral centre.

2 · Surveillance

  • Active (house-to-house fever search by MPW/ASHA) + passive (fever cases at facilities).
  • Case-based notification and investigation; “test–treat–track”.
  • Indicators: API, ABER, SPR, SfR.

3 · Integrated vector management

  • IRS — indoor residual spraying (DDT, malathion, synthetic pyrethroids).
  • LLINs — long-lasting insecticidal nets for high-risk areas.
  • Anti-larval: source reduction, temephos/biolarvicides, larvivorous fish (Gambusia, guppy).
  • Personal protection — repellents, screens, full sleeves.

4 · Supportive

  • Epidemic preparedness & rapid response.
  • BCC/IEC — Anti-Malaria Month (June), World Malaria Day (25 April).
  • Inter-sectoral convergence (irrigation, urban bodies, tribal welfare).
  • Capacity building, monitoring & evaluation, operational research.

Outcome

📉 Cases & deaths

4.6 Social Health Programme

Book section 4.6 · Not one single scheme — a family of programmes that improve health by improving people's social conditions and community action.

🤝 Story mode — Big Hero 6

Baymax alone can't save San Fransokyo — it takes the whole team: Go Go, Wasabi, Honey Lemon, Fred and Hiro, each fixing a different problem. Social health works the same way. Health doesn't only come from hospitals; it comes from food, schooling, a girl's education, clean water, a toilet, a pension, a smoke-free kitchen, insurance, and a trusted village worker who links families to care. Every one of those is a “team member” — a social health programme.

📘 Exam definitions

Social health is the social dimension of WHO's definition of health — a person's ability to form satisfying relationships, adapt to social situations and live as a productive member of the community; for a community, it means adequate social conditions (income, education, housing, social security, equity).

Social health programmes are organised, usually government-led, community-based programmes that improve the health of the population by acting on the social determinants of health — poverty, nutrition, education, gender, water & sanitation, housing and social security — and by mobilising community participation.

Objectives

  1. Reduce health inequalities between rich and poor, rural and urban, men and women.
  2. Improve nutrition, sanitation, housing and education — the “causes behind the causes” of disease.
  3. Provide social security & financial protection against health costs.
  4. Empower women and vulnerable groups; promote community participation.
  5. Link the community with the health system (outreach workers).

4.7 Role of WHO in Indian National Programmes

Book section 4.7 · WHO is India's technical partner — the Genie who doesn't do the work for you, but makes your wishes possible.

🧞 Story mode — Aladdin

The Genie can't make anyone fall in love or bring back the dead — he has rules. But within those rules he hands Aladdin knowledge, tools and phenomenal cosmic power. India belongs to the South-East Asia Region (SEARO), whose regional office is in New Delhi; the WHO Country Office for India is also in New Delhi.

Guidelines, training, programme reviews, health statistics, research.

Q. Discuss in detail the various National Family Welfare Programmes.
Essay · 10 marks · KUHS Model paper · ~25 min · ~3 pages · (also covers Dec 2023 short note)
Definitions (1m) → History milestones (2m) → Objectives incl. NPP 2000 (2m) → Methods tree diagram (2m) → Services & schemes (2m) → Outcome + pharmacist + conclusion (1m)

1. Introduction & definitions

India was the first country in the world to launch a national family planning programme (1952). Family planning (WHO, 1971) is a way of thinking and living adopted voluntarily, on the basis of knowledge, attitudes and responsible decisions by individuals and couples, to promote the health and welfare of the family and the social development of the country. Family welfare is broader — family planning integrated with maternal & child health, nutrition, immunisation and health education.

2. Evolution (milestones)

  • 1952 — National Family Planning Programme (clinic approach) in the 1st Five-Year Plan.
  • 1960s — extension-education approach; Department of Family Planning (1966).
  • 1971 — MTP Act.
  • 1977 — renamed Family Welfare Programme; strictly voluntary.
  • 1996 — Target-Free Approach → Community Needs Assessment Approach (1997).
  • 1997 — merged into RCH programme; 2000 — National Population Policy.
  • 2005 — NRHM; 2013 — part of RMNCH+A under NHM.
  • 2016 — Mission Parivar Vikas (146 high-fertility districts, 7 states); 2017 — Antara & Chhaya added.

3. Objectives

  1. Reduce birth rate to reach replacement TFR 2.1 and stabilise population.
  2. Promote small family norm voluntarily.
  3. Ensure spacing between births; delay first pregnancy.
  4. Reduce MMR & IMR by integrating FP with MCH.
  5. Provide a wide basket of free contraceptive choices and meet unmet need.

NPP 2000 — immediate: meet unmet needs for contraception, infrastructure and personnel, integrated RCH services; medium-term: replacement TFR by 2010; long-term: stable population by 2045. It set 14 socio-demographic goals (IMR < 30, MMR < 100, 80% institutional deliveries, universal immunisation, girls' marriage after 18, 100% registration of vital events…).

5. Services & schemes

  • Free contraceptives at all public facilities; home delivery of contraceptives by ASHA; Ensuring Spacing at Birth counselling.
  • Post-partum FP (PPIUCD, post-partum sterilisation); fixed-day services.
  • Compensation for sterilisation acceptors; Family Planning Indemnity Scheme.
  • Mission Parivar Vikas for high-TFR districts; FP-LMIS logistics; World Population Day & Vasectomy fortnights; IEC on small family norm.
  • Supportive laws: MTP Act 1971 (amended 2021), PC-PNDT Act 1994 against sex selection.

6. Outcome

TFR has fallen to 2.0 (NFHS-5 & NFHS-6) — below replacement; contraceptive prevalence 66.7% (NFHS-5) → 69.1% (NFHS-6); unmet need ≈ 9%. Gaps: over-reliance on female sterilisation, low male participation, high TFR in Bihar/UP/Meghalaya.

Counselling on OCP use and missed pills, emergency contraception, condom use for dual protection; myth-busting; maintaining contraceptive stocks; reporting ADRs.

From a target-driven sterilisation programme India has moved to a voluntary, rights-based, target-free family welfare programme integrated in RMNCH+A — and has achieved replacement-level fertility.

Q. National malaria prevention programme — objectives, strategies and outcome. Predicted
Essay 10 / Short 5 · also feeds the 4× “prevention & control of malaria” PYQs (May 22, Dec 23, Jul 23, Model)

1. Introduction

Malaria is a vector-borne protozoal disease caused by Plasmodium (mainly P. vivax and P. falciparum in India) and transmitted by the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. [Draw Fig 4.5]

2. Evolution

NMCP 1953 → NMEP 1958 → Modified Plan of Operation 1977 → Malaria Action Programme / Enhanced Malaria Control Project (1990s) → NAMP 1999 → NVBDCP 2003 (6 VBDs) → National Framework for Malaria Elimination 2016–2030 → National Strategic Plans 2017–22 & 2023–27. NVBDCP directorate is now NCVBDC.

3. Goals & objectives (NFME)

Zero indigenous cases throughout India by 2030 and prevent re-establishment. Objectives: eliminate from 26 Category 1 & 2 states/UTs by 2022; incidence < 1/1000 everywhere by 2024; interrupt transmission in all states incl. Category 3 by 2027; malaria-free status maintained by 2030. Categories 0–3 by API. [Fig 4.6]

4. Strategies

  1. Early diagnosis & complete treatment — RDT/microscopy; Pv: chloroquine 3 d + primaquine 14 d; Pf: ACT-SP (ACT-AL in NE) + single-dose primaquine; severe: injectable artesunate.
  2. Surveillance — active & passive case detection, case investigation; API, ABER, SPR indicators.
  3. Integrated vector management — IRS, LLINs, anti-larval measures (source reduction, temephos, biolarvicides, Gambusia/guppy fish), personal protection.
  4. Epidemic preparedness & rapid response.
  5. BCC/IEC — World Malaria Day (25 April), Anti-Malaria Month (June).
  6. Inter-sectoral convergence, capacity building, M&E, research.

5. Outcome

Reported cases fell ≈ 80% (2015 → 2023) and deaths ≈ 78%; WHO estimates fell from 6.4 million (2017) to 2 million (2023); India exited WHO's High Burden to High Impact group in 2024.

6. Pharmacist & conclusion

Ensure drug/RDT stocks, counsel on completing 14-day primaquine, watch for haemolysis in G6PD deficiency, discourage monotherapy. Conclusion: India has moved from control to elimination and is on track for a malaria-free 2030.

Burden (1m) → Laws: COTPA/FCTC (1m) → Objectives (1m) → Three-tier implementation + diagram (1.5m) → Cessation/outcome (0.5m)

Burden: 28.6% of Indian adults (≈ 26.7 crore) use tobacco (GATS-2, 2016–17); about 13.5 lakh deaths a year. Legal base: COTPA 2003 (Sec 4 no public smoking; Sec 5 no ads; Sec 6a no sale to under-18; Sec 6b none within 100 yards of educational institutions; Sec 7 pictorial warnings, 85% since 2016), WHO FCTC (ratified 2004), PECA 2019 (e-cigarette ban).

NTCP (2007–08) objectives: awareness; reduce production & supply; implement COTPA; help quitting; implement FCTC strategies.

Implementation: National (NTCC, MoHFW) — policy, mass-media campaigns, tobacco testing labs, research on alternative crops/livelihoods, surveillance (GATS). State (STCC) — advocacy, training of trainers, law-enforcer sensitisation. District (DTCC) — training of health/social workers and teachers, IEC, school programmes, monitoring COTPA, tobacco cessation centres, Panchayat involvement. [Draw Fig 4.4]

Cessation: Quitline 1800-11-2356, mCessation; NRT, bupropion, varenicline; pharmacists use the 5 A's. Outcome: prevalence 34.6% → 28.6% between GATS-1 and GATS-2.

Extra 2-markers from question banks

Toxic / harmful effects of tobacco

Nicotine → addiction, tachycardia; tar → cancers (oral, lung, larynx, oesophagus, bladder); CO → hypoxia, CHD, low birth weight; COPD, stroke, peptic ulcer; smokeless → leukoplakia, oral submucous fibrosis, oral cancer; passive smoking harms others.

Sources (forms) of tobacco

Smoked: cigarette, bidi, cigar, hookah, chutta (reverse smoking). Smokeless: khaini, gutkha, zarda, paan with tobacco, mishri, gul, snuff. Newer: e-cigarettes/heated tobacco (banned 2019).

Infant & neonatal mortality rate

IMR = infant (<1 y) deaths per 1000 live births (27, SRS 2021). NMR = deaths within 28 days per 1000 live births (19). Both are sensitive indicators of health status and MCH services.

Intervention programmes for children

UIP + Mission Indradhanush, HBNC, HBYC, SNCU, IMNCI, RBSK (4 Ds), ICDS, POSHAN Abhiyaan, Anemia Mukt Bharat, deworming (NDD), PM POSHAN.

JSY vs JSSK

JSY (2005): cash incentive for institutional delivery (demand side). JSSK (2011): free entitlements — delivery, C-section, drugs, diagnostics, diet, blood, transport (supply side).

COTPA 2003 (in 4 lines)

Central tobacco law: Sec 4 public-place smoking ban; Sec 5 advertising ban; Sec 6(a) & (b) sales to minors and near schools banned; Sec 7 mandatory pictorial health warnings.
