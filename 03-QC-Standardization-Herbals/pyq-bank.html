How to write each type in QC Herbals

📕 Essay (10 m)

  1. Definition with its source (WHO / EU / ICH / D&C Act) and year.
  2. 6–8 underlined headings in the order of the guideline.
  3. One flowchart or labelled diagram (HPTLC steps, GACP chain, stability timeline) and one table (limits, zones, documents).
  4. A herbal example (e.g. curcuminoids in turmeric, withanolides in ashwagandha).
  5. Two-line conclusion: why it protects the patient.

📒 Short note (5 m)

  1. Definition (1 mark).
  2. Procedure as numbered steps, or 5–8 points under 2–3 headings.
  3. Formula or small table: % ash, % extractive, stomatal index, lycopodium formula, Rf value.
  4. One line of significance or example.

📗 Brief (2 m)

  1. Exact definition (1 mark).
  2. Two examples, a formula, or 2–3 bullets (1 mark).
  3. No long introductions: 4–5 lines and move on.

How to write a 10-mark answer (draw this plan in your head)

Worked plan for KUHS Model Q1 “Discuss the WHO guidelines for quality control of herbal drugs”: introduction (WHO QC methods manual 1998, updated 2011; aim = identity, purity, quality) → table of parameters → macroscopy & microscopy → foreign matter, ash, extractive, moisture → swelling, foaming, bitterness, haemolytic activity, tannins → contaminants (pesticides, heavy metals, microbes, aflatoxins, radioactivity) with a limits table → TLC fingerprint → flowchart “sample → identity → purity → safety → assay” → conclusion.

7 🏰 Exam-Hall Tips

⏱️

Run the clock

Read all 19 questions first and tick the ones you know. Then essays → short notes → briefs. Checkpoints: 0:50 essays done · 2:15 short notes done · 2:50 briefs done · last 10 min revision. All questions are compulsory: never leave a blank.
📅

Quote the source and the year

WHO QC methods 1998 (updated 2011) · ICH 1990 · WHO safety & efficacy research guidelines 1993 · DSHEA 1994 · WHO GACP 2003 · EU THMPD 2004/24/EC and HMPC 2004 · WHO PV guidelines for herbals 2004 · WHO GMP for herbal medicines 2007 · Schedule T under Rule 157.
🧮

Drop in a formula

% foreign matter, % ash and % extractive (all on air-dried basis) · stomatal index S ÷ (E + S) × 100 · lycopodium % purity = N × W × 94,000 × 100 ÷ (S × M × P) · Rf = distance moved by spot ÷ distance moved by solvent front.
✏️

Draw for marks

HPTLC step flow · HPLC block diagram · TLC plate with Rf · GACP chain · stability timeline & climatic-zone table · ash-value flowchart · the four stomata types · a regulation comparison table (India / USA / EU).
🌿

Always name a herb

Turmeric (curcuminoids) · ashwagandha (withanolides) · senna (sennosides) · liquorice (glycyrrhizin) · kalmegh (andrographolide) · St John's wort (hyperforin) · ispaghula (swelling index) · ginkgo (bleeding with warfarin).
🍎

Use the exact words

Write “identity, purity, quality, safety”, “traditional herbal medicinal product”, “well-established use”, “active vs analytical marker”. And never write that herbals are safe because they are natural: the poisoned apple looked natural too!
