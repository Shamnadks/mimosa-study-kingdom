📘 Exam definition (write exactly this)

A National Health Programme is a nationwide, centrally planned public-health activity launched by the Government of India (Ministry of Health & Family Welfare) with clear objectives, targets, strategies, staffing and funding, to prevent and control a disease or health problem of national importance. Today most of them run under the umbrella of the National Health Mission (NHM), implemented through State and District Health Societies.

1 HIV & AIDS Control Programme (NACP) — guarded by Elastigirl 🎗️

The most-asked topic of the unit: two full essays, one model short note, two briefs. Learn it as the 5-phase staircase.

🎬 Story mode

In Incredibles 2, Elastigirl becomes the public face of the campaign to make supers legal again: she talks to the media, changes how people think, and stretches into every corner of the city. That's NACP. It fights an enemy that hides quietly in the blood for years, it reaches out to the people at highest risk instead of waiting for them, it fights stigma with awareness, and like Helen holding on to baby Jack-Jack, it protects every newborn from getting the virus from mum (PPTCT).

📘 Exam definition

The National AIDS Control Programme (NACP) is a centrally funded national programme, run by the National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO) under MoHFW, that aims to prevent new HIV infections and to provide care, support and free treatment to people living with HIV (PLHIV), through State AIDS Control Societies and district units.

🦸‍♀️ Hero File · NACP (Elastigirl)
📅 Launch yearFirst Indian HIV case detected in 1986 (Chennai); NACP launched 1987; NACO set up 1992 and Phase I began. Phases: I 1992–99 · II 1999–2006 · III 2007–12 · IV 2012–17 (extended to 2021) · V 2021–26.
🏛️ Nodal bodyNACO (MoHFW) → SACS (State AIDS Control Societies) → DAPCU (District AIDS Prevention & Control Units) → service points (ICTC, ART centres, TIs). Phase V is a Central Sector Scheme fully funded by GoI (₹15,471.94 crore).
🎯 Objectives(1) Prevent new infections in high-risk groups and the general population · (2) Provide care, support & treatment to all PLHIV · (3) Strengthen infrastructure, systems and human resources down to the district · (4) Build a strong strategic-information (surveillance, M&E) system · (5) Remove stigma and discrimination.
⚙️ FunctioningPrevention (targeted interventions, condoms, STI care, blood safety, IEC) → Testing (ICTC, community screening, PPTCT) → Treatment (free ART, viral-load testing) → Care & support → Surveillance & research.
🧰 Key schemesFree ART (1 Apr 2004) · Test & Treat (2017) · PPTCT · Link Worker Scheme · Red Ribbon Clubs · Suraksha (STI) clinics · Opioid Substitution Therapy · Sampoorna Suraksha Kendras (Phase V single-window centres) · Mission Sampark · helpline 1097 · HIV/AIDS (Prevention & Control) Act 2017.
📈 Outcome / targets2024 estimates: adult prevalence 0.20%; 64,500 new infections (↓49% since 2010); 32,200 AIDS deaths (↓81.4%). Phase V target: 80% cut in new infections and AIDS deaths by 2025-26 vs 2010; dual elimination of mother-to-child transmission of HIV & syphilis; zero stigma; aligned with SDG 3.3 (end AIDS as a public-health threat by 2030).
💊 Pharmacist's roleART adherence counselling, side-effect and interaction checks, condom/needle supply, PEP referral within 72 h, OST dispensing, confidential non-judgemental counter, blood-donation awareness.

🦠

Agent

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV-1 mostly), a retrovirus that destroys CD4 T-cells → AIDS.
❤️‍🩹

Sexual

Unprotected sex: the commonest route in India.
🩸

Blood & needles

Unscreened blood, shared needles/syringes (injecting drug users).
🤱

During pregnancy, delivery or breastfeeding.

Psst, Mimosa… HIV does NOT spread by hugging, shaking hands, sharing food, toilets or mosquito bites. Write that line: it's an anti-stigma point examiners love. 💕

How NACP functions (structure → services)

🛡️ 1. Prevention (the force field)

  • Targeted Interventions (TI) through NGOs for high-risk groups: female sex workers, men who have sex with men, injecting drug users, transgender people, plus bridge populations (truckers, migrants).
  • Link Worker Scheme for rural, vulnerable villages.
  • Condom promotion (free + social marketing).
  • STI/RTI control at Suraksha clinics (an STI raises HIV risk).
  • Blood safety: voluntary donation only; every unit screened (HIV, HBV, HCV, syphilis, malaria).
  • Harm reduction: needle–syringe exchange and Opioid Substitution Therapy (buprenorphine / methadone).
  • IEC: mass media, Red Ribbon Clubs in colleges, workplace programmes.

💊 2. Testing, treatment & care

  • ICTC (Integrated Counselling & Testing Centres): voluntary, confidential testing with pre- and post-test counselling.
  • PPTCT: test every pregnant woman; lifelong ART for positive mothers; ARV prophylaxis for the baby.
  • Free ART at ART centres and Link ART centres; Test & Treat: ART starts on diagnosis, whatever the CD4 count.
  • Dolutegravir-based first-line regimen; routine viral-load monitoring.
  • HIV–TB coordination: every PLHIV screened for TB and vice versa.
  • Care & Support Centres, nutrition and social-welfare linkage.
  • PEP after exposure (start ideally within 2 h, max 72 h; 28 days).

📊 3. Strategic information

HIV Sentinel Surveillance, HIV estimations, M&E systems, research. Tells NACO where to focus.

⚖️ 4. Legal & social shield

HIV/AIDS (Prevention & Control) Act, 2017: bans discrimination (jobs, schools, healthcare, housing, insurance), needs informed consent for testing, protects confidentiality of HIV status, appoints an ombudsman in each state.

💊 Pharmacy example

A young man buys an HIV self-test kit and looks scared. A good pharmacist speaks privately, explains that a reactive self-test must be confirmed at the nearest ICTC (free and confidential), mentions helpline 1097, and, if he describes a risky exposure in the last 72 hours, sends him straight to a hospital for PEP. No lecture, no judgement: that's Elastigirl-level outreach.

2 National TB Programme (NTP → RNTCP → NTEP) — guarded by Mr. Incredible 🫁

One essay (Dec 2023) and one short note (Jul 2023). The story is a 60-year upgrade, from a district clinic model to a digital “End TB” mission.

🎬 Story mode

Mr. Incredible is the strongest of the family, but his real superpower is endurance. Remember him holding up a falling train, refusing to let go? TB treatment is that train: six months of daily tablets, and if the patient lets go after two months because the cough stopped, the bacteria come back stronger (drug-resistant TB). So the programme's whole design is about helping people not let go: free drugs, a treatment supporter, money for food, reminders on the phone.

📘 Exam definition

The National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP), formerly the Revised National TB Control Programme (RNTCP), is the centrally sponsored programme of MoHFW (Central TB Division) that provides free diagnosis, free treatment and patient support for all TB patients in India, with the goal of ending TB by 2025, five years ahead of the SDG target of 2030.

💪 Hero File · TB Programme (Mr. Incredible)
📅 Launch yearNTP 1962 (District TB Centre model + BCG) → RNTCP (pilot 1993, launched 1997 with DOTS; whole country covered by the mid-2000s) → NSP 2017–25 → renamed NTEP on 1 Jan 2020 → PM TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (9 Sep 2022) → 100-day intensified campaign (from 7 Dec 2024).
🏛️ Nodal bodyCentral TB Division, MoHFW → State TB Cell → District TB Centre → TB Units → Designated Microscopy / NAAT centres → PHC, Ayushman Arogya Mandir, ASHA.
🎯 Objectives(1) Detect all TB cases early, including private-sector patients · (2) Treat every patient with quality-assured drugs and support until cure · (3) Prevent new infections (TB preventive therapy, BCG, airborne infection control) · (4) Build systems: Ni-kshay, staff, community partnerships. These are the 4 pillars of the National Strategic Plan 2017–25: Detect – Treat – Prevent – Build (DTPB).
⚙️ FunctioningPresumptive TB → free sputum test (NAAT/smear) + chest X-ray → notification on Ni-kshay → universal drug-susceptibility testing → free daily fixed-dose combination (FDC) treatment → follow-up → cure → TB preventive therapy for contacts.
🧰 Key schemesNi-kshay (web-based patient management; Ni = end, Kshay = TB) · Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana (nutrition DBT, ₹500/month from 2018, raised to ₹1,000/month in 2024) · Ni-kshay Mitra (community donors adopt patients) · TB Mukt Panchayat · 99DOTS adherence tech · Patient Provider Support Agencies for private patients · call centre Ni-kshay Sampark 1800-11-6666.
📈 Outcome / targetsNSP 2025 targets vs 2015: incidence 217 → 44 per lakh (~80% ↓), mortality 32 → 3 per lakh (~90% ↓), zero catastrophic cost to families. WHO (2015 → 2024): incidence ↓ 21% (237 → 187 per lakh), deaths ↓ 28%. Progress is real, but the 2025 target was not fully met.
💊 Pharmacist's roleAnti-TB drugs are in Schedule H1 (keep a register); notify TB patients; spot coughers ≥2 weeks and refer; adherence counselling; warn about side-effects (orange urine with rifampicin, liver toxicity, isoniazid neuropathy → pyridoxine, ethambutol eye effects); act as DOT/treatment supporter.

From DOTS to NTEP — what changed?

📚 Textbook: RNTCP + DOTS (1997)

  • Sputum smear microscopy for diagnosis
  • Intermittent (thrice-weekly) regimens
  • Directly Observed Treatment by a DOT provider
  • Goal: cure ≥85% of new smear-positive cases, detect ≥70%
VS

🆕 Now: NTEP (2020 →)

  • Rapid molecular tests (CBNAAT/Truenat) and universal DST
  • Daily FDC regimen by weight band
  • Treatment supporter + digital adherence (99DOTS)
  • Nutrition DBT, Ni-kshay Mitra, private-sector notification
  • Goal: end TB, not just control it

A woman on rifampicin panics because her urine is orange and her oral contraceptive may fail. 3 Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) — guarded by Agent Rick Dicker 📡

Asked as a 2-marker (Dec 2023: “concept of IDSP”), but it's a favourite of the question banks as a short note too.

🎬 Story mode

Agent Rick Dicker sits in his dim government office surrounded by screens and incident files. Every time a super causes trouble anywhere in the country, a report lands on his desk, and he quickly sends a team to clean up. Swap “super incidents” for “disease outbreaks” and that's IDSP: every health worker, doctor and lab sends a weekly report, the surveillance unit spots an unusual rise, and a Rapid Response Team rushes out before the outbreak spreads.

📘 Exam definition

Disease surveillance is the continuous, systematic collection, analysis and interpretation of health data, and its timely sharing with those who must act. IDSP is India's decentralised, laboratory-supported, IT-enabled surveillance system for epidemic-prone diseases, which detects outbreaks in their early rising phase and responds through trained Rapid Response Teams.

🕴️ Hero File · IDSP (Agent Rick Dicker)
📅 Launch yearNovember 2004, with World Bank support (as the Integrated Disease Surveillance Project); continued under NHM with domestic funds. Digital upgrade: Integrated Health Information Platform (IHIP) launched 5 April 2021.
🏛️ Nodal bodyCentral Surveillance Unit (CSU) at the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), Delhi → State Surveillance Units (SSU) → District Surveillance Units (DSU) → reporting units (sub-centres, PHCs, CHCs, hospitals, labs).
🎯 Objectives(1) Strengthen a decentralised, lab-based, IT-enabled surveillance system for epidemic-prone diseases · (2) Monitor disease trends · (3) Detect outbreaks early and respond through trained RRTs · (4) Provide data for planning.
⚙️ FunctioningThree levels of case definition: S (syndromic) by health workers, P (presumptive) by doctors, L (lab-confirmed) by laboratories → reported weekly (Monday–Sunday) → DSU analyses → early warning signal → RRT investigates and controls → feedback.
🧰 Key componentsIntegration & decentralisation of surveillance units · human-resource training · IT network (IDSP portal → IHIP real-time, geo-tagged reporting) · strengthening public-health laboratories · media scanning & verification of rumours.
📈 OutcomeHundreds of outbreaks (cholera, dengue, measles, food poisoning, Nipah, chikungunya…) detected and controlled every year; backbone of India's COVID-19 data response; IHIP shifts from paper forms to near real-time digital reporting.
💊 Pharmacist's roleNotice clusters (sudden rush for ORS, antipyretics, anti-diarrhoeals) and alert the PHC/DSU; stock outbreak drugs; supply data; share health messages during outbreaks.

The S-P-L reporting pyramid

🧑‍⚕️

S form — Syndromic

Filled by health workers using simple symptom groups (e.g., fever with rash, cough >2 weeks, acute flaccid paralysis). Most sensitive, earliest.
🩺

P form — Presumptive

Filled by medical officers from clinical diagnosis (e.g., suspected typhoid, malaria, measles).
🔬

L form — Lab-confirmed

Filled by laboratories after testing. Most specific: confirms the cause.
🧠 Mnemonic

See it (symptoms) → Probably it (doctor's guess) → Locked in (lab proof). Dicker's file gets thicker at each step!

⭐ Exam tip

For the 2-mark “concept of IDSP”: definition + launched Nov 2004 + S/P/L forms + weekly reporting + RRT + IHIP 2021. Six keywords, six ticks.

⚠️ Spelling

KUHS printed it as “IDP”. Always write the full form Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP) so the examiner sees you know it.

4 National Leprosy Control / Eradication Programme (NLCP → NLEP) — guarded by Frozone 🧊

Asked as an essay (Jul 2023), twice as a short note and once as a brief in the model paper. A top-3 topic.

🎬 Story mode

Frozone freezes everything he touches. Now imagine a pale patch on the skin that feels as if it's been frozen: touch it with cotton and the person feels nothing. That's the classic sign of leprosy: an anaesthetic, hypopigmented patch. The bacteria love cool areas (skin, peripheral nerves, nose). If treatment is late, the nerves die and hands and feet get injured without the person feeling it, which leads to deformity. Frozone's famous shout “Where is my super suit?!” becomes your exam shout: “Where is my MDT blister pack?!” Free MDT for every patient is the heart of the programme.

📘 Exam definition

Leprosy (Hansen's disease) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae, mainly affecting the skin, peripheral nerves, eyes and nasal mucosa. The National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP) is a centrally sponsored programme (under NHM) that provides free early detection, free Multi-Drug Therapy (MDT), disability prevention and rehabilitation, with the aim of reaching zero transmission.

❄️ Hero File · Leprosy Programme (Frozone)
📅 Launch yearNLCP 1955 (dapsone monotherapy; Survey–Education–Treatment) → NLEP 1983 (MDT introduced) → leprosy eliminated as a public-health problem nationally in Dec 2005 (prevalence <1 per 10,000) → National Strategic Plan & Roadmap 2023–27: zero transmission by 2027.
🏛️ Nodal bodyCentral Leprosy Division (DGHS, MoHFW) → State Leprosy Officer → District Leprosy Officer / nucleus team → PHC, Ayushman Arogya Mandir, ASHA. Integrated with general health services under NHM.
🎯 Objectives(1) Early detection of all cases, especially hidden ones · (2) Complete free MDT and cure · (3) Prevent disability and rehabilitate (DPMR) · (4) Reduce stigma and discrimination · (5) Interrupt transmission (contact screening + prophylaxis).
⚙️ FunctioningActive case finding (house-to-house Leprosy Case Detection Campaigns, ASHA searches) → diagnosis by cardinal signs → classify PB/MB → free MDT blister packs → monitor reactions → DPMR (self-care, MCR footwear, reconstructive surgery) → contact screening + single-dose rifampicin PEP.
🧰 Key schemesSPARSH Leprosy Awareness Campaign (from 30 January, Anti-Leprosy Day) · Leprosy Case Detection Campaign · SDR-PEP (single-dose rifampicin post-exposure prophylaxis for contacts) · Nikusth digital case-tracking platform · welfare allowance for reconstructive surgery · ASHA incentives.
📈 Outcome / targetsElimination (PR <1/10,000) achieved Dec 2005 and sustained nationally; target now zero transmission by 2027. 🆕 From 1 April 2025, paucibacillary (PB) patients also get the 3-drug MDT (uniform regimen).
💊 Pharmacist's roleRecognise and refer “painless pale patches” (don't sell steroid creams for them!), counsel on MDT adherence and harmless discolouration, report reactions, supply self-care kits, fight stigma.

Diagnosis: the 3 cardinal signs (any ONE = leprosy)

⚪

1. Skin patch

Hypopigmented or reddish patch with definite loss of sensation.
🦾

2. Nerve

Thickened/enlarged peripheral nerve with sensory loss or muscle weakness.
🔬

3. Smear

Acid-fast bacilli in a slit-skin smear.

PB vs MB classification

🔹 Paucibacillary (PB)

  • 1–5 skin lesions

What

A special drive launched in December 2014 to reach children and pregnant women who were unvaccinated or partially vaccinated, focusing on low-coverage districts and urban slums. The seven rainbow colours stood for the seven diseases targeted at launch.

🧊

Temperatures

🔥

❄️

📦 Open-vial policy (one line each)

1. Introduction

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a retrovirus that destroys CD4 T-lymphocytes; its late stage is AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome).   • Provide greater care, support and free treatment to all people living with HIV (PLHIV).

  • Strengthen the nationwide strategic-information management system (surveillance, M&E, research).
  • Phase V: reduce annual new infections and AIDS-related deaths by 80% by 2025-26 (baseline 2010); dual elimination of vertical transmission of HIV and syphilis; eliminate HIV/AIDS-related stigma and discrimination; universal access to STI/RTI services; end AIDS as a public-health threat by 2030 (SDG 3.3).

Q. Explain in detail about National leprosy control program.

1. Introduction

Leprosy (Hansen's disease) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae, affecting mainly the skin, peripheral nerves, eyes and nasal mucosa. It spreads by droplets from untreated multibacillary patients after prolonged close contact; the incubation period is long (several years). If untreated it causes nerve damage and deformity, and it carries heavy social stigma.

Cardinal signs (one is enough for diagnosis): (1) hypopigmented/reddish skin patch with definite loss of sensation; (2) thickened peripheral nerve with sensory loss or weakness; (3) acid-fast bacilli in slit-skin smear.

2. History

  • 1955 – National Leprosy Control Programme (NLCP): dapsone monotherapy; Survey, Education and Treatment (SET).
  • 1983 – National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP): Multi-Drug Therapy (MDT) introduced.
  • December 2005: leprosy eliminated as a public-health problem at national level (prevalence <1 per 10,000).
  • National Strategic Plan & Roadmap 2023–27: zero transmission of leprosy by 2027.
  • 1 April 2025: uniform 3-drug MDT for paucibacillary patients too.

3. Objectives of NLEP

  • Early detection and complete treatment of all leprosy cases with free MDT.
  • Reduce the disease burden and interrupt transmission (contact tracing, chemoprophylaxis).
  • Prevent disabilities and provide medical rehabilitation (DPMR).
  • Reduce stigma and discrimination through IEC.
  • Integrate leprosy services with general health care and build capacity of staff.

🟢 Answer briefly (2 marks)

Q. Mention the objectives of pulse polio program.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS May 2022 · 3–5 lines

Pulse Polio (launched 1995) gives 2 drops of OPV to all children 0–5 years on National Immunization Days. Objectives: (1) immunise every under-five child irrespective of previous doses; (2) interrupt transmission of wild poliovirus and eradicate polio; (3) maintain polio-free status (certified 2014) until global eradication; (4) strengthen routine immunization and AFP surveillance; (5) generate community awareness and participation.

Q. National AIDS control program.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS May 2022 · 3–5 lines

NACP was launched in 1987 and is implemented by NACO (set up 1992) through SACS and DAPCUs. It aims to prevent new HIV infections and provide care, support and free treatment to PLHIV. Main components: targeted interventions, condom promotion, blood safety, STI care, ICTC and PPTCT, free ART (since 2004; Test & Treat 2017) and the HIV/AIDS Act 2017. Current Phase V (2021–26) targets an 80% fall in new infections and AIDS deaths.

Q. Prevention of polio myelitis.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · 3–5 lines
  • Routine immunization: OPV at birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks and booster at 16–24 months; fractional IPV at 6 and 14 weeks (+ 9 months since 2023).
  • Pulse Polio: OPV to all under-fives on NIDs/SNIDs, house-to-house coverage, mop-up rounds.
  • AFP surveillance in children under 15 years + environmental (sewage) surveillance.
  • Safe water, sanitation, hand-washing (faeco-oral route) and health education.
Q. Concept of mental health.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · 3–5 lines

Mental health (WHO) is a state of well-being in which a person realises their own abilities, copes with the normal stresses of life, works productively and contributes to the community; it is not merely the absence of mental illness. A mentally healthy person (1) feels comfortable about himself, (2) feels right towards others and (3) is able to meet the demands of life. Mental health lies on a continuum and is shaped by biological, psychological and social factors.

Q. What is universal immunization program.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · 3–5 lines

UIP is the national programme launched in 1985 (from the EPI of 1978) that gives free vaccines to all children and pregnant women against 12 vaccine-preventable diseases — TB, polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Hib, measles, rubella, rotavirus diarrhoea, pneumococcal pneumonia and (in endemic districts) Japanese encephalitis — through a fixed national schedule and cold chain. Mission Indradhanush (2014) strengthens it.

Q. Discuss the functions of national program for deafness.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · 3–5 lines

NPPCD (pilot 2007, 25 districts) functions through 4 components: (1) manpower training of ENT doctors, audiologists and health workers; (2) capacity building of ENT/audiology services at district hospital, CHC and PHC; (3) service provision: screening (newborn, school), early treatment of ear disease, surgery and free hearing aids/rehabilitation; (4) awareness generation through IEC/BCC to prevent avoidable hearing loss.

Q. Discuss the concept of Integrated Disease Surveillance Program (IDSP).
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · 3–5 lines

IDSP (launched November 2004) is a decentralised, lab-supported, IT-enabled surveillance system for epidemic-prone diseases. Data are collected weekly at three levels — S (syndromic, health workers), P (presumptive, doctors) and L (lab-confirmed) — and analysed by District/State/Central Surveillance Units (CSU at NCDC) so that outbreaks are detected early and controlled by Rapid Response Teams. Reporting is now digital on IHIP (2021).

Q. Mention the prevention measures of HIV infection.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · 3–5 lines
  • Safe sex: abstinence, mutual faithfulness, correct and consistent condom use (ABC).
  • Safe blood: voluntary donation and screening of every unit.
  • No sharing of needles/syringes; needle exchange and opioid substitution therapy for drug users.
  • PPTCT: testing of pregnant women and ART for mother and baby.
  • Early testing and treatment (ART suppresses virus), PEP within 72 h, treatment of STIs, health education.
Q. Discuss the functions of National program for control of blindness.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Jul 2023 · 3–5 lines

NPCB (1976; NPCB&VI from 2017) aims to reduce avoidable blindness. Functions: free cataract surgery with IOL; school eye screening and free spectacles; free near-vision spectacles for the elderly; eye banking and eye donation; vision centres at PHCs and strengthening of district/medical-college eye units; management of glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy and childhood blindness; training and IEC.

Q. National Leprosy Control Programme.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Model paper · 3–5 lines

NLCP was launched in 1955 using dapsone monotherapy and became the National Leprosy Eradication Programme in 1983 with Multi-Drug Therapy (rifampicin, clofazimine, dapsone). Strategies: early case detection, free MDT, disability prevention and rehabilitation, IEC against stigma. Leprosy was eliminated as a public-health problem (prevalence <1/10,000) in December 2005; the target now is zero transmission by 2027.
