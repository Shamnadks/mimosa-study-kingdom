1 Principles of Formulation & Building Blocks

Before any single recipe, learn the "rules of the kitchen" that every product follows.

🎬 Story mode: Tiana's recipe card

Tiana never starts cooking by grabbing random jars. First she decides who she is cooking for and what the dish must do, then picks a base (stock), the star ingredient, helpers (thickener, seasoning, preservative salt), checks they don't curdle together, tastes for feel and flavour, writes the method (what to heat, what to add last) and finally checks the cost and the health inspector's rules. A cosmetic formulator follows exactly these steps.

📘 Exam definition: building blocks

The building blocks of a cosmetic are the functional classes of ingredients that together make the product: the vehicle/base (water, oils, alcohol), active ingredients (which deliver the claimed benefit) and excipients — surfactants/emulsifiers, rheology modifiers, humectants, emollients, preservatives, antioxidants, chelating agents, pH adjusters, colours and fragrances. Principles of formulation are the rules for choosing and combining these blocks so the product is effective, safe, stable, pleasant to use, economical and legally compliant.

Emulsions: the base of most creams

An emulsion is a dispersion of one liquid as droplets in another immiscible liquid, stabilised by an emulsifier. The outer (continuous) phase decides how the product feels.

Identify the type: dilution test (O/W mixes with water), dye test (water-soluble amaranth colours the continuous phase of O/W; oil-soluble Sudan III colours W/O), conductivity (O/W conducts electricity).

2 Face Wash

A gentle cleanser that removes dirt, sweat, sebum and make-up without stripping the acid mantle.

🎬 Story mode

After a long night of cooking, Tiana washes her face. Soap from the kitchen leaves her skin tight and squeaky (too alkaline, too stripping). A good face wash is the "gentle broth": mild surfactants that lift the grease, a humectant so the skin doesn't feel dry, and a pH close to the skin's own (about 5.5).

📘 Exam definition

Face wash is a rinse-off facial cleansing preparation (gel, cream, foam or liquid) containing mild surfactants in an aqueous base, designed to remove dirt, excess sebum, sweat, pollutants and make-up from the face while maintaining the skin barrier and pH; it may carry actives for specific skin types (e.g. salicylic acid for oily, acne-prone skin).

Types

Foaming gel face wash · cream (lotion) cleanser · foaming (pump) cleanser · micellar water · cleansing milk · scrub/exfoliating face wash · medicated/anti-acne face wash · herbal face wash (neem, tulsi, aloe).

Ideal properties

  • Cleanses effectively but mildly; does not leave skin tight or dry
  • pH close to skin (≈ 5.5–7); non-irritant to skin and eyes; non-comedogenic
  • Good, creamy foam; rinses off easily without residue
  • Pleasant fragrance and appearance; stable; microbiologically safe

Sensory feel, advantages & disadvantages

Sensory: fresh, clean, lightly foaming; gel types feel cool and "squeaky clean"; cream cleansers feel softer and suit dry skin.

👍 Advantages

  • Milder than soap; pH can match skin
  • Hygienic (no shared bar), convenient
  • Can carry actives for each skin type
  • Removes make-up and pollutants quickly
  • Pleasant foam and fragrance
VS

👎 Disadvantages

  • Costlier than soap
  • Water base needs preservatives
  • Strong surfactants/over-use → dryness, irritation
  • Actives (salicylic acid) may sting sensitive skin
  • Plastic packaging waste
💊 Pharmacy example

An anti-acne face wash with 2% salicylic acid is a classic cosmeceutical use of the face-wash base; a benzoyl-peroxide wash is a drug product.

3 Moisturizing Cream

Nov 2022 essay: "materials used in manufacturing of moisturizing cream". Know every class in the table below.

🎬 Story mode

Tiana's beignet dough dries and cracks if left open. She brushes it with a little honey (pulls water in = humectant), a little butter (softens = emollient) and covers it with a cloth (stops water escaping = occlusive). A moisturizing cream does all three jobs for dry skin.

📘 Exam definition

Moisturizing creams are emulsions (usually O/W; W/O for very dry skin) that increase and maintain the water content of the stratum corneum and restore its barrier, by combining occlusive agents (reduce TEWL), humectants (attract water), emollients (smooth and soften) and sometimes barrier-repair actives (ceramides, NMF components), leaving the skin soft, smooth and supple.

How a moisturizer works

🛡️

Occlusion

Oily film slows TEWL (petrolatum, dimethicone, beeswax)
🧲

Humectancy

Draws water into SC (glycerin, urea, hyaluronic acid)
🪶

Emolliency

Fills gaps, smooths roughness (IPM, shea butter)
🧱

Barrier repair

Supplies "mortar" lipids (ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids)

4 Cold Cream

Elsa's touch: rich, white and refreshingly cool the moment it meets the skin.

🎬 Story mode

When Elsa touches something, it turns cool. Cold cream does a tiny version of that trick: it is a W/O emulsion that breaks on the warm skin, and the released water evaporates, taking heat away — a pleasant cooling sensation. Then the oil left behind dissolves make-up and leaves the skin soft and protected, like a winter cloak.

📘 Exam definition

Cold creams are emollient cleansing creams, classically water-in-oil emulsions made of beeswax, mineral oil and water emulsified by the soap formed in situ from borax and the free fatty acids of beeswax. They give a cooling effect on application due to evaporation of water and are used for cleansing (removing make-up), massage and softening dry skin. The idea dates back to the Greek physician Galen (2nd century AD), who mixed beeswax, olive oil and rose water.

Beeswax–borax chemistry

2 RCOOH (free fatty acids of beeswax, e.g. cerotic acid) + Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O (borax) → 2 RCOONa (sodium soap, emulsifier) + 4 H₃BO₃ (boric acid) + 5 H₂OThe sodium soap formed in situ, together with beeswax esters, emulsifies the system. With a high oil phase the emulsion is W/O; with more water it can invert to O/W.
  • Borax must be balanced against beeswax — only part of the free acid is neutralised. In the classic formula below, borax ≈ 1/16 of the beeswax.
  • Excess borax → free borax/boric acid may crystallise (grainy cream), pH rises, irritation risk.
  • Too little borax → insufficient soap → coarse, unstable emulsion that "sweats" oil.

Sensory, advantages & disadvantages (Nov 2022)

Sensory: thick, white, rich and glossy; immediate cooling feel; spreads with an oily slip; leaves a shiny, greasy, protective film.

👍 Advantages

  • Cooling, soothing on application
  • Excellent cleanser — oils dissolve make-up and oily grime
  • Strong emollient & occlusive → good for dry skin and winter
  • Good massage cream (long slip)
  • Simple, cheap ingredients and easy hot process
  • Protects skin from cold, wind and chapping
VS

👎 Disadvantages

  • Greasy, sticky, heavy; shiny look
  • Unsuitable for oily and acne-prone skin (may block pores)
  • Not easily washed off with water (W/O)
  • May stain clothes and pillows
  • Natural oils/waxes can go rancid → need antioxidants
  • Borax/boric acid: irritation risk; grainy texture if excess
  • Emulsion may bleed oil at high temperature (must pass BIS thermal stability)
⚠️ Common mistake

Saying cold cream "contains ice" or "is stored cold". The cooling is due to evaporation of the water released when the emulsion breaks on warm skin.

5 Vanishing Cream

The Cheshire Cat of creams: it disappears into the skin and leaves only a smile — a dry, matte, non-greasy film.

🎬 Story mode

In Alice in Wonderland, the Cheshire Cat fades away until only his grin is left. Rub a vanishing cream on the skin and the same thing happens: the water evaporates, the cream seems to vanish, and what remains is an invisible thin film of stearic acid crystals that looks matte and holds powder beautifully.

📘 Exam definition

Vanishing creams are oil-in-water emulsions based on stearic acid, a part of which (about 15–25%) is saponified by an alkali (KOH, NaOH, triethanolamine) to form a stearate soap that emulsifies the remaining free stearic acid. On application they spread and appear to "vanish", leaving a non-greasy, dry, matte film. They are used as day creams, foundation creams and powder bases.

C₁₇H₃₅COOH (stearic acid) + KOH → C₁₇H₃₅COOK (potassium stearate, emulsifier) + H₂OOnly part of the acid is neutralised; the free stearic acid crystallises as fine plates → characteristic pearly sheen ("pearlescence") that develops in 24–48 h.

Method (Model paper: "formulation and preparation"): (1) Melt stearic acid and cetyl alcohol at 75–80 °C (oil phase). (2) Dissolve KOH, glycerin and methylparaben in water heated to the same temperature (aqueous phase). (3) Add the hot aqueous phase gradually to the melted acid with constant stirring — saponification forms potassium stearate and the O/W emulsion forms. (4) Continue slow stirring until cool (avoid incorporating air). (5) Add perfume at about 40 °C. (6) Allow to stand 24–48 h for pearlescence to develop, then fill into jars.

Formulation tips

  • Choice of alkali: KOH gives the smoothest cream; NaOH gives a harder, more brittle cream; mixtures are common; triethanolamine gives very soft creams.
  • Degree of saponification controls consistency: more soap → softer, more alkaline cream.
  • Humectant prevents surface drying in the jar and reduces "rolling" (white flakes when rubbed).
  • Use pure stearic acid with low iodine value (less oxidation, whiter cream).

Sensory, advantages & disadvantages (May 2022)

Sensory: light, white, pearly; spreads quickly, absorbs/"vanishes" in seconds; dry, matte, non-greasy after-feel; slight "powdery" smoothness.

👍 Advantages

  • Non-greasy, non-sticky, invisible film
  • Excellent base for face powder & make-up (foundation cream)
  • Matte finish — ideal for oily/normal skin and humid climates like Kerala
  • O/W → easily washable with water
  • Cheap, simple ingredients; easy to perfume and colour
  • Good vehicle for water-soluble actives and sunscreens
VS

👎 Disadvantages

  • Little emollient/occlusive action → not enough for very dry skin or winter
  • Alkaline pH (soap) may dry or irritate sensitive skin
  • May dry out in the container (water loss) or show "rolling"
  • Texture sensitive to quality of stearic acid and degree of saponification
  • Not compatible with acid actives (AHAs) or electrolytes (soap breaks)

7 Hair Care Principles · Shampoo & Conditioning Shampoo

Model-paper essay: "Classify shampoo. Describe the ingredients used in the formulation of shampoo."

🎬 Story mode

Rapunzel's 70-foot hair needs washing! A plain shampoo would clean it but leave it squeaky, tangled and full of static. A conditioning shampoo is Tiana's two-in-one pot: the surfactant team lifts the sebum and dust, and while she rinses, tiny conditioning particles drop out of the lather and stick to the hair, so it comes out clean and smooth.

Principles of formulating hair-care products

  • Clean the hair and scalp without over-stripping sebum (hair should not become dry and brittle).
  • Respect the negative charge of hair: cationic ingredients deposit to condition; avoid excess alkali (swells the cuticle).
  • Keep pH mildly acidic to neutral (≈ 5–7) to keep cuticles flat and shiny; non-irritant to eyes and scalp.
  • Good sensory: rich lather, easy rinsing, wet and dry combability, gloss, manageability, pleasant fragrance.
  • Stability (no separation of silicones, pearl, actives), microbiological safety, regulatory compliance (IS 7884 shampoo, IS 7123 hair oils, Cosmetics Rules 2020).

Calling dandruff a "bacterial" problem. It is linked mainly to the yeast Malassezia + sebum + individual sensitivity. Leave the shampoo on the scalp for 3–5 minutes so the active can work.

9 Hair Conditioners

Ariel's dinglehopper detangles — conditioners do it chemically.

🎬 Story mode

Ariel combs her hair with a fork she calls a "dinglehopper". Without a conditioner, wet hair snags, frizzes and builds static. A conditioner coats each hair with a slippery, positively charged layer so the comb glides through — no dinglehopper drama.

📘 Exam definition (Nov 2022 / Model brief)

Hair conditioners are preparations applied after (or with) shampooing that deposit cationic surfactants/polymers, fatty alcohols, silicones, oils and proteins on the hair to reduce static and friction, detangle, smooth the cuticle and improve softness, shine and manageability. Examples: cetrimonium chloride / behentrimonium chloride rinse-off conditioners, silicone serums, leave-in sprays, protein masks.

Role / functions

🪮 Easier wet and dry combing (detangling)
⚡ Neutralise static charge, reduce fly-away
✨ Smooth cuticle → shine, gloss
🧸 Softness, body, manageability
🛡️ Protect from heat, UV, mechanical damage; reduce split ends
💧 Replenish lipids and moisture to dry, damaged hair

Mechanism: hair keratin carries negative charges (more in damaged hair). Positively charged heads of quaternary compounds and cationic polymers are attracted electrostatically and adsorb, with their fatty tails facing out to form a lubricating, hydrophobic film (Fig 2.3). Fatty alcohols + cationics form a lamellar gel network that gives the creamy texture; silicones add slip and shine.

Types

Rinse-off (cream rinse) · leave-in (spray, lotion) · deep/intensive conditioners and hair masks · protein conditioners · hair serums (silicone-based) · 2-in-1 conditioning shampoos.

11 Hair Dyes

Essay in Jul 2023 AND Dec 2023, brief in May 2022. This is the single most-asked Unit 2 topic.

🎬 Story mode

When Rapunzel's hair is cut, it turns from glowing gold to brown in a moment. Real hair colour changes need chemistry. A temporary dye is like paint on the tower wall (washes off in the rain); a semi-permanent dye is like a stain that soaks into the outer bricks and fades over weeks; a permanent dye smuggles tiny colourless "builders" inside the tower where they join into huge coloured blocks too big to escape through the door.

📘 Exam definition (May 2022 brief)

Hair dyes are cosmetic preparations used to change the natural colour of hair — to cover grey hair, to restore, deepen or lighten the shade, or for fashion colours. They act either by depositing colour on the cuticle, by diffusing small dyes into the cortex, or by forming large dye molecules inside the cortex by oxidation (permanent oxidative dyes based on p-phenylenediamine and hydrogen peroxide).

Ideal properties of a hair dye

  • Harmless to hair, scalp and skin; non-toxic and non-sensitising
  • Gives a uniform, natural-looking shade on grey and pigmented hair
  • Fast to washing, light, sweat, friction and perming (for permanent types)
  • Does not stain the scalp excessively; easy to apply in reasonable time
  • Stable during storage (no premature oxidation); compatible with other hair treatments

Chemistry of permanent (oxidative) hair dyes

  1. Alkalising agent (ammonia or monoethanolamine, pH 9–10.5) swells the hair and lifts the cuticle; it also activates H₂O₂.
  2. Primary intermediates ("bases": PPD, p-toluenediamine, p-aminophenol) and couplers ("modifiers": resorcinol, m-aminophenol, m-phenylenediamine, 1-naphthol) — small, colourless — diffuse into the cortex.
  3. H₂O₂ oxidises the primary intermediate to a reactive quinonediimine; at the same time it lightens natural melanin.
  4. The quinonediimine reacts with a coupler to form a colourless leuco compound, which is further oxidised to large indo dyes (indamines, indophenols — di- and tri-nuclear).
  5. These large molecules cannot diffuse out → permanent colour. The final shade depends on the combination of intermediates and couplers (e.g. PPD + resorcinol → greenish-brown; PPD + m-aminophenol → brown/magenta tones; PPD + m-phenylenediamine → blue).

Hydrogen peroxide strength: "20 volume" = about 6% H₂O₂ (1 mL releases 20 mL oxygen); 30 vol ≈ 9%, 40 vol ≈ 12% (used for more lift).

12 Oral Care Principles · Toothpaste Building Blocks

May 2022 essay + Jul 2023 short + model brief — oral care is guaranteed marks.

🎬 Story mode

Cinderella's smile at the ball came from somewhere: a paste that polishes like fine sand, foams to reach every corner, tastes of fresh mint, never dries in the tube and quietly drops fluoride into the enamel to make it acid-proof. Each of those jobs is one building block.

📘 Exam definitions (IS 6356 wording, simplified)

A dentifrice is any substance or combination of substances specially prepared for cleaning the accessible surfaces of teeth. A toothpaste is a dentifrice in the form of a smooth, semi-solid, homogeneous mass containing abrasives/polishing agents, surface-active agents, humectants, binding agents and other substances for oral health, which can be extruded from its container.

Principles of formulation of oral-care products

  1. Safety if swallowed — low toxicity, permitted ingredients (IS 4707), no mono- or disaccharides such as sucrose (IS 6356), fluoride within limits (Cosmetics Rules 2020: ≤ 1000 ppm F, ppm stated on tube and carton).
  2. Effective cleaning with minimum abrasion — abrasive harder than stains but softer than enamel; low dentine abrasivity (IS 6356 type test, ≤ 2.5 × reference); fine particles.
  3. Therapeutic benefit — anti-caries, antiplaque, desensitising, anti-tartar, whitening, as claimed.
  4. Compatibility — e.g. calcium abrasives bind fluoride from NaF → pair CaCO₃/DCP with sodium monofluorophosphate; NaF with silica; chlorhexidine is inactivated by SLS.
  5. Stability — no phase separation, hardening or gas; extrudable after heat (45 °C, 28 days) and cold (5 °C) storage; tube must not corrode.
  6. Consumer acceptance — pleasant flavour and sweetness, mouthfeel, foam, smooth texture, attractive colour/stripes.
  7. Mildness to gums and oral mucosa; pH 5.5–10.5 (IS 6356).
  8. Packaging, cost, regulatory compliance.

1. Definition

Hair dyes are cosmetic preparations used to change the colour of hair — to cover grey hair, restore or deepen natural colour, lighten it or give fashion shades — by depositing colour on the cuticle, diffusing dyes into the cortex, or forming large dye molecules inside the cortex by oxidation.

Ideal properties: non-toxic, non-irritant and non-sensitising; uniform natural shade; fast to washing, light, sweat and friction; does not damage hair or stain scalp; stable on storage; easy and quick to apply; compatible with other hair treatments.

2. Classification

  1. Temporary dyes — large acid dyes/pigments coat the cuticle; removed by one shampoo (colour rinses, sprays, gels, hair mascara).
  2. Semi-permanent dyes — small pre-formed nitro and anthraquinone dyes (HC Red 3, HC Yellow 2, 2-nitro-p-phenylenediamine) diffuse into the outer cortex without developer; last about 6–8 shampoos.
  3. Demi-permanent dyes — mild oxidative dyes with low peroxide and no ammonia; last several weeks.
  4. Permanent (oxidative) dyes — PPD-type precursors + couplers + H₂O₂ in alkaline medium; colour formed inside cortex; lasts until hair grows out.
  5. Vegetable dyes — henna (lawsone), indigo, chamomile (apigenin), walnut (juglone).
  6. Metallic (progressive) dyes — lead acetate, silver nitrate, bismuth citrate; form metal sulphides gradually (lead/arsenic compounds are prohibited for colouring cosmetics under the Cosmetics Rules 2020).
  7. Hair bleaches — H₂O₂ + ammonia ± persulphates.

3. Chemistry of permanent oxidative dyes

Draw Fig 2.4. Ammonia (pH 9–10.5) swells the hair and lifts the cuticle → small colourless primary intermediates (p-phenylenediamine, p-toluenediamine, p-aminophenol) and couplers (resorcinol, m-aminophenol, m-phenylenediamine, 1-naphthol) penetrate the cortex → H₂O₂ oxidises the intermediate to a reactive quinonediimine and simultaneously bleaches melanin → quinonediimine couples with the coupler to give leuco compounds that are oxidised to large coloured indo dyes (indamines, indophenols) → these are too big to diffuse out, so the colour is permanent. Shade depends on the intermediate–coupler combination (e.g. PPD + resorcinol → greenish-brown; PPD + m-phenylenediamine → blue).

Q. Formulation and advantages of vanishing creams / Explain the formulation and preparation of vanishing cream.

Vanishing creams are O/W emulsions of stearic acid partly (≈ 15–25%) saponified by KOH/NaOH/TEA; the stearate soap emulsifies the free stearic acid, which is left on the skin as a thin, invisible, matte film. C₁₇H₃₅COOH + KOH → C₁₇H₃₅COOK + H₂O.

Advantages: non-greasy, non-sticky, invisible matte finish; excellent foundation and powder base; suits oily/normal skin and humid climates; easily washable (O/W); cheap and simple; good vehicle for water-soluble actives and sunscreens. (Disadvantages: little emollience for dry skin, alkaline pH, may dry or "roll".)

Method: melt oil phase at 75–80 °C; dissolve KOH + glycerin in hot water; add aqueous to oil with stirring; cool; perfume at 40 °C; age 24–48 h.

B. Cold cream (W/O)

Beeswax + mineral oil + water emulsified by sodium soap formed in situ from the free fatty acids of beeswax and borax: 2 RCOOH + Na₂B₄O₇·10H₂O → 2 RCOONa + 4 H₃BO₃ + 5 H₂O. Cools the skin by evaporation of water. Formula: white beeswax 16, light liquid paraffin 50, borax 1, methylparaben 0.15, perfume q.s., water to 100. Method: melt beeswax with paraffin at 70–75 °C; dissolve borax and preservative in water at 75 °C; add aqueous phase slowly to the oil with continuous stirring; stir till cool; perfume at 40 °C; fill. Borax must be balanced against beeswax — excess gives crystals/grainy cream, too little an unstable emulsion.

Q. Discuss in detail various advantages and disadvantages of cold cream.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · ~12 min
Definition + composition (1m) → Advantages (2m) → Disadvantages (2m)

Cold cream is a W/O emollient cleansing cream of beeswax, mineral oil, borax and water that produces a cooling sensation on application because the water released evaporates. Typical formula: beeswax 16, liquid paraffin 50, borax 1, water 33, perfume, preservative.

Advantages

  1. Pleasant cooling and soothing effect.
  2. Excellent cleanser — the oil phase dissolves make-up, sebum and oily dirt.
  3. Good emollient and occlusive; relieves dry, rough, chapped skin; protective in cold, windy weather.
  4. Ideal massage cream due to long slip.
  5. Cheap, readily available ingredients; simple process.
  6. Oily base dissolves lipid-soluble actives (vitamin E, almond oil) for night creams.

Disadvantages

  1. Greasy, heavy, sticky; leaves a shiny film.
  2. Not suitable for oily or acne-prone skin; may block pores.
  3. W/O — difficult to wash off with water; stains clothes.
  4. Natural fats and waxes may turn rancid; needs antioxidants.
  5. Borax/boric acid may irritate; excess borax gives a grainy cream.
  6. Emulsion can separate (bleed oil) in hot climates; must pass the BIS thermal-stability test.
Q. Explain the formulation of anti-dandruff shampoos. Write a note on its building blocks.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · ~12 min
Dandruff + definition (1m) → Actives table (1.5m) → Building blocks (1.5m) → Formula + method (1m)

Dandruff is excessive visible scaling of the scalp associated with the yeast Malassezia, sebum and individual sensitivity. Anti-dandruff shampoos contain antifungal, cytostatic or keratolytic agents in a shampoo base.

Actives: zinc pyrithione ~1% (antifungal), ketoconazole 1–2% (azole antifungal; 2% medicated), selenium sulphide 1–2.5% (antifungal + cytostatic), climbazole ~0.5%, piroctone olamine 0.5–1%, coal tar (cytostatic), salicylic acid and sulphur (keratolytic).

Building blocks: primary anionic surfactants (SLES, ALS) — cleanse sebum and flakes; secondary surfactants (CAPB, cocamide MEA) — mildness, foam; suspending agents (carbomer, acrylates copolymer, glycol distearate) — keep insoluble ZPT/Se sulphide dispersed; deposition/conditioning polymers (guar HPTC, PQ-10); soothing agents (menthol); thickener (NaCl); pH adjuster; preservative; strong perfume; water.

Formula: SLES 28% 35, CAPB 7, cocamide MEA 1.5, ZPT 48% 2.1 (≈1%), glycol distearate 1.5, acrylates copolymer 0.8, guar HPTC 0.2, menthol 0.1, sodium benzoate 0.5, perfume 0.8, NaCl and pH adjuster q.s., water to 100. Method: hydrate polymers, add SLES, melt in MEA and EGDS at 75 °C, add betaine, cool, add ZPT dispersion below 40 °C with gentle mixing (avoid iron contact), add menthol/perfume and preservative, adjust pH and viscosity.

Q. Explain formulation considerations for toothpaste.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Jul 2023 · ~12 min
Definition (0.5m) → Considerations (2.5m) → Ingredients list (1.5m) → Manufacture in 2 lines (0.5m)

A toothpaste is a smooth semi-solid dentifrice containing abrasives, humectants, binders and surfactants that cleans the accessible surfaces of teeth.

  1. Abrasivity: clean stains without wearing enamel/dentine — fine particles, low RDA; fineness and gritty-particle limits (IS 6356).
  2. Consistency & stability: binder–humectant balance so paste does not separate, harden or dry; extrudable at 5 °C and stable at 45 °C.
  3. Compatibility: fluoride source vs abrasive (NaMFP with CaCO₃/DCP; NaF with silica); cationics not with SLS; tube inertness.
  4. Fluoride level: ≤ 1000 ppm, declared on tube and carton (Cosmetics Rules 2020).
  5. Taste & mouthfeel: flavour, non-cariogenic sweeteners (no sucrose), adequate foam.
  6. Safety: non-irritant to mucosa, low toxicity if swallowed, pH 5.5–10.5, heavy metals ≤ 20 ppm Pb, ≤ 2 ppm As.
  7. Microbial quality: preservatives; total viable count ≤ 1000/g.
  8. Specific claims: desensitising, antiplaque, whitening actives at effective levels.

Ingredients: abrasive 20–50%, humectant 10–40%, binder 0.5–2%, surfactant 1–2%, flavour 0.5–1.5%, sweetener, fluoride, opacifier (TiO₂), preservative, colour, water. Manufacture: gel of binder in humectant + water → add salts → add abrasive under vacuum → surfactant → flavour → de-aerate → fill.

Q. Explain the principle involved in the formulation of face wash / hair conditioners.

📐 Principles

Purpose → building blocks (base + active + helpers) → compatibility → stability → safety → sensory → process → packaging → regulatory → cost. Hot process: oil 70–75 °C + water 75 °C → emulsify → cool → perfume at 40 °C → fill.

🧼 Face wash · 💧 Moisturizer

Face wash: SLES/isethionate + CAPB, glycerin, NaCl, actives, pH 5.5–7. Moisturizer: occlusive (petrolatum) + humectant (glycerin) + emollient (IPM) + emulsifier (GMS, polysorbate 60) + fatty alcohol + preservative/antioxidant; O/W.

❄️ Cold vs 🐱 Vanishing

Cold: W/O, beeswax 16 + paraffin 50 + borax 1 + water 33; cooling, greasy, cleansing, dry skin. Vanishing: O/W, stearic acid 17 + KOH 0.7 + glycerin 8 + water; matte, non-greasy, powder base, oily skin; pearlescence 24–48 h.

🧴 Shampoo

Classify by form / function / base (IS 7669 soap, IS 7884 surfactant). SLES (primary) + CAPB, cocamide MEA (secondary) + PQ-10/guar, silicone + NaCl + EGDS + EDTA + citric acid + preservative + perfume. Coacervate deposition on dilution.

❄️ Anti-dandruff · 🪮 Conditioner · 🥥 Oils

Malassezia. ZPT ~1%, ketoconazole 2%, Se sulphide 1–2.5%, climbazole, piroctone; suspending agent. Conditioner: behentrimonium/cetrimonium + cetearyl alcohol + silicone + PQ + pH 3.5–5. Oils: coconut, sesame, paraffin + herbs + BHT + perfume (IS 7123).

🎨 Hair dyes

Temporary (acid dyes) · semi-permanent (nitro dyes) · demi · permanent (PPD + coupler + NH₃ + H₂O₂ 6%) · vegetable (henna) · metallic (lead acetate — prohibited in India) · bleach. QDI → indo dye trapped. Two packs mixed 1:1. Patch test 24 h (Cosmetics Rules 2020).

🦷 Toothpaste

Abrasive 20–50 · humectant 10–40 · binder 0.5–2 · SLS 1–2 · flavour 0.5–1.5 · saccharin · NaF 0.22 / NaMFP 0.76 (≈ 1000 ppm, India max) · TiO₂ · preservative · water. Vacuum mixing. Sensitive: KNO₃ 5% / SrCl₂ 10%. Bleeding gums: zinc citrate, SnF₂, CPC, alum, neem, clove.

✨ Whitening · 🫗 Mouthwash

Whitening: silica, polyphosphates, blue covarine; HP, carbamide peroxide (10% ≈ 3.5% HP); strips, trays, pens. Mouthwash: water, ethanol, glycerin, poloxamer, CHX 0.12–0.2% / CPC 0.05–0.1% / essential oils, NaF 0.05%, zinc, menthol, saccharin.
