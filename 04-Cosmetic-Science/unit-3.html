1 The Sun, UV Rays & Sun Protection

Before we choose an armour, we must know the enemy: which rays, how deep they go, and what they do.

🎬 Story mode

Moana's canoe is far from Motunui and the sun is overhead. Some of its rays never reach her: the sky's ozone shield swallows them (that's UVC). Some are short and fierce and turn her nose red by evening (UVB, the burning rays). The sneakiest ones are long and quiet: they slip through clouds and even through glass, sink deep into her skin and, over years, would wrinkle it like Gramma Tala's (UVA, the ageing rays). Sun protection is her plan against all three.

📘 Exam definitions

Ultraviolet radiation (UVR) is the part of sunlight with wavelengths shorter than visible light (about 100–400 nm). It is divided into UVC, UVB and UVA.

Sun protection is the use of behaviour (avoiding peak sun, shade), clothing (hats, sunglasses) and topical products (sunscreens) to prevent the harmful effects of UVR on skin: sunburn, tanning, photo-ageing and skin cancer.

How deep does each ray go?

Effects of UV on skin

🔥

Sunburn (erythema)

Redness, pain, blistering after over-exposure, mainly UVB; appears within hours and peaks around a day later.
🟤

Tanning

Immediate pigment darkening (minutes, UVA, oxidation of existing melanin) → persistent pigment darkening → delayed tanning (new melanin after 2–3 days, UVB + UVA).
👵

Photo-ageing

Wrinkles, leathery skin, sagging, age spots (lentigines), dilated vessels; mainly UVA through ROS and collagen-degrading enzymes.
🧬

Skin cancer

Basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma; UVB damages DNA directly, UVA indirectly.
🛡️

Immunosuppression

UV dampens skin immunity (Langerhans cells), favouring infections such as cold sores and tumour growth.
⚡

Photosensitivity

Phototoxic and photo-allergic rashes with some drugs (tetracyclines, sulphonamides) and perfumes (bergamot oil).

A = Ageing (long, deep, all year)B = Burning (short, surface)C = Cancelled by ozone

"Moana's sun: Ageing, Burning, Cancelled."

2 Sunscreens & Their Classification

KUHS has asked "classify sunscreen preparations" in four papers: learn this section until you can draw the tree with your eyes closed.

🎬 Story mode

Moana has two kinds of armour. The first is like Maui's shiny fish-hook held up as a mirror: tiny white mineral particles sit on the skin and bounce and scatter the rays away (physical sunscreens). The second is like the coconut-husk sponge on her canoe: special molecules soak up the UV energy and let it out as a little harmless warmth (chemical sunscreens). Smart sailors wear both.

📘 Exam definition

Sunscreens are topical preparations containing substances (UV filters) that protect the skin from the harmful effects of solar ultraviolet radiation by reflecting/scattering (physical) or absorbing (chemical) the radiation before it reaches living skin cells.

Ideal properties of a sunscreen

🌈

Broad protection

Absorbs/blocks the whole 290–400 nm range (UVB + UVA).
☀️

Photostable

Does not break down in sunlight.
🤲

Safe

Non-toxic, non-irritant, non-sensitising, not absorbed into the blood.
💧

Substantive

Water- and sweat-resistant; stays on skin.
✨

Cosmetically elegant

Non-greasy, no white cast, no staining, pleasant odour.
🧪

Compatible & stable

Mixes with other ingredients and packaging; economical.

Mechanism: shield vs sponge

🪞 Physical (inorganic) sunscreens

  • Insoluble mineral powders: zinc oxide, titanium dioxide (main), kaolin, talc, calamine, iron oxides, magnesium oxide, red veterinary petrolatum
  • Reflect and scatter UV (and also absorb part of it); act immediately
  • Broad (ZnO covers UVB + full UVA; TiO₂ mainly UVB + UVA-II)
  • Photostable, rarely irritant, good for sensitive skin and children
  • Drawback: white cast, opaque, heavy; reduced by micronised/coated grades
VS

🧽 Chemical (organic) sunscreens

  • Organic molecules with conjugated aromatic rings (chromophores)
  • Absorb UV and release it as heat
  • Each covers a band (UVB, UVA-II or UVA-I) so they are combined
  • Invisible, light, elegant; easy to formulate
  • Drawbacks: some photo-unstable (avobenzone), may sensitise (PABA, benzophenones), need ~15–30 min to form an even film

Formulation pointers + one sample formula

  • Combine filters (UVB + UVA + inorganic) for broad-spectrum cover; add a photostabiliser (octocrylene for avobenzone).

  • Add antioxidants (vitamin E acetate) and soothers (aloe, allantoin); keep pH skin-friendly; preserve the water phase.

💊 Pharmacy example

A dermatologist prescribing tretinoin for acne or hydroquinone for melasma always adds "broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning", because both make skin more sun-sensitive and UV undoes the treatment. Sunscreens are sold as cosmetics in India and the EU but as OTC drugs in the USA.

⚠️ Common mistake

Don't write "physical = absorbs". Physical/inorganic = reflects and scatters; chemical/organic = absorbs. (Newer studies show micronised ZnO/TiO₂ also absorb much of the UV, so "mainly reflect and scatter, partly absorb" is the safest wording.)

3 Sun Protection Factor (SPF)

One number on every sunscreen bottle, three exam questions every year. Learn the definition word for word.

🎬 Story mode

On the open sea, Moana's bare shoulder turns pink after 10 minutes. With her SPF 15 coconut lotion it takes about 15 times longer, in theory 150 minutes, before the same faint pink appears. That ratio, "how many times longer before I go red", is the SPF. Heihei, who ate half the lotion, is not protected at all. 🐔

📘 Exam definition (write exactly this)

Sun Protection Factor (SPF) is the ratio of the minimal erythemal dose (MED) of UV radiation on sunscreen-protected skin to the MED on unprotected skin, when the product is applied at 2 mg/cm².

Minimal erythemal dose (MED) is the smallest dose of UV radiation that produces the first perceptible, clearly defined redness (erythema) of the skin, read 16–24 hours after exposure.

SPF = MED (protected skin) ÷ MED (unprotected skin) SPF mainly measures protection against UVB-induced sunburn
% UVB blocked = (1 − 1/SPF) × 100 SPF 15 → 93.3 % · SPF 30 → 96.7 % · SPF 50 → 98 %

From EU Commission Recommendation 2006/647/EC (non-binding, widely followed): a sun product should have SPF ≥ 6, its UVA protection factor should be at least one-third of the labelled SPF and its critical wavelength at least 370 nm. Japan and many Asian brands (common in India) show UVA strength as PA+ to PA++++ (based on persistent pigment darkening). In the USA, "water resistant (40 min)" or "(80 min)" may be claimed after testing.

Determination of SPF

🧑‍🔬 In vivo (on human volunteers)

  • Reference/"gold standard": ISO 24444:2019
  • Measures real erythema → MED
  • Accurate for label claims
  • Slow, costly, ethical issues (deliberately inducing sunburn), subjective reading
VS

🧪 In vitro (in the lab)

  • Spectrophotometric: Mansur method (UV absorbance of a dilute solution)
  • Thin-film transmittance: Diffey–Robson (product spread on tape/PMMA plate)
  • 🆕 ISO 23675:2024 Double Plate Method (no volunteers)
  • Quick, cheap, no ethics issues; good for screening

A. In vivo method (ISO 24444 principle)

Water resistance is tested the same way after the volunteers sit in water for set periods (e.g. 2 × 20 min for a "40 minutes" claim, 4 × 20 min for "80 minutes").

C. In vitro: thin-film transmittance (Diffey–Robson) & newer ISO methods

The product is spread at about 2 mg/cm² on a roughened substrate (surgical tape or PMMA plate) to imitate skin; UV transmittance T(λ) from 290–400 nm is measured with a spectroradiometer/integrating sphere and SPF is calculated as the ratio of erythemally weighted UV reaching unprotected vs protected skin: SPF = ∫E(λ)S(λ)dλ ÷ ∫E(λ)S(λ)T(λ)dλ (E = erythema action spectrum, S = solar spectrum). The same set-up gives the UVA-PF and critical wavelength (ISO 24443). 🆕 In December 2024 ISO published two new SPF methods: ISO 23675 (in vitro "double plate" method using moulded + sandblasted PMMA plates) and ISO 23698 (hybrid diffuse reflectance), as alternatives to testing on volunteers.

Significance of SPF

📏

Measure of efficacy

Tells how well a product protects against sunburn (UVB).
🛒

Helps choose

Pick SPF by skin type and exposure: daily office use vs beach/trekking.
⚖️

Compare products

Standard number lets consumers and pharmacists compare brands.
🏷️

Label claim / regulation

Basis of SPF categories and claims; must be tested by standard methods.
🩺

Health

Higher SPF + broad spectrum reduces risk of sunburn, photo-ageing and skin cancer.
🧪

Formulation tool

In vitro SPF guides the formulator in choosing and combining filters.
⚠️ Limits of SPF (great for extra marks)

SPF says nothing about UVA (that needs UVA-PF/PA/critical wavelength); it assumes 2 mg/cm² while people apply much less; it is reduced by sweat, water and rubbing; and it is not linear (SPF 50 is not twice SPF 25).

4 Role of Herbs in Cosmetics

Grandmother Willow's lesson: nature already made most of the beauty actives we need; the cosmetic scientist just learns which leaf does what.

🎬 Story mode

Pocahontas never bought a cream in her life. She healed scratches with a cool green gel, washed her long hair with plant pastes and chewed twigs to clean her teeth. Meeko keeps stealing the turmeric, Flit guards the clove buds. Each plant in her forest is a whole cosmetic factory: an active, a colour, a perfume and a preservative in one leaf.

📘 Exam definition

Herbal cosmetics are cosmetic preparations in which one or more herbs (plant parts, extracts, juices, oils or isolated phytoconstituents) are used, either as the main active for a cosmetic or cosmeceutical benefit, or as a functional excipient (colour, perfume, humectant, thickener, preservative), in a suitable base.

Roles herbs play in a formulation

✨

Actives (cosmeceutical)

Anti-acne (neem, turmeric), anti-dandruff (neem, henna), anti-ageing antioxidants (amla, green tea), soothing (aloe), hair tonic (amla, bhringraj).
🎨

Colourants

Henna (lawsone), turmeric (curcumin), indigo, annatto, beetroot, saffron.
🌸

Perfumes & flavours

Clove (eugenol), rose, sandalwood, lavender, mint, cinnamon in toothpastes.
💧

Humectants & emollients

Aloe gel, plant glycerin; vegetable oils (coconut, almond, sesame), shea and cocoa butter.
🧴

Vehicles, thickeners

Aloe gel as base; gums (acacia, tragacanth, guar, xanthan), pectin, starch, plant waxes.
🛡️

Preservatives & antioxidants

Neem, clove, rosemary, tocopherols, grapefruit-seed and green tea extracts.
🫧

Natural cleansers

Saponin herbs: reetha (soapnut), shikakai (Acacia concinna) in herbal shampoos.
☀️

Sun care

Antioxidants (green tea, amla, turmeric) and soothers (aloe) as add-ons to sunscreens.
🦷

Oral care

Neem, clove, babool, miswak, mint, tulsi in toothpastes and mouthwashes.

👍 Advantages of herbal cosmetics

  • Generally mild, fewer side effects; consumer trust ("natural")
  • Multifunctional: one herb = active + colour + perfume + preservative
  • Rich traditional (Ayurvedic) evidence; cheap, renewable
  • Antioxidant and nutritive (vitamins, minerals)
  • Growing export and "green" market
VS

👎 Limitations

  • Variable composition (place, season, storage) → needs standardisation
  • Microbial contamination, pesticide and heavy-metal residues
  • Colour, odour and stability problems; shorter shelf life
  • "Natural" ≠ safe: allergy (clove oil, henna mixtures), phototoxicity
  • Fewer controlled clinical studies; slow action

1. Introduction

Herbal cosmetics are products in which plant materials (powders, extracts, gels, oils or isolated constituents) are incorporated as actives or functional excipients in a suitable base. Herbs are used because they are mild, multifunctional, rich in vitamins, antioxidants and antimicrobial principles, and backed by long traditional (Ayurvedic) use.

Roles of herbs in cosmetics: (i) actives with cosmeceutical benefits (anti-acne, anti-ageing, soothing, anti-plaque); (ii) natural colours (turmeric, henna); (iii) perfumes and flavours (clove, mint, rose); (iv) humectants and emollients (aloe gel, vegetable oils); (v) vehicles and thickeners (aloe gel, gums); (vi) preservatives and antioxidants (neem, clove, rosemary, vitamin E).

2. Herbs in skin care

(a) Aloe

Predicted Essay · 10 marks · Mock Paper A · ~25 min · ~3 pages
UV + definition (1m) → classification tree with examples (3m) → SPF definition + formula (1.5m) → method: in vivo or Mansur with formula/table (3m) → photo-ageing note (1.5m). Diagrams: Fig 3.1 spectrum, Fig 3.4 tree, Fig 3.6 test grid.

1. Introduction

Solar UV is divided into UVC (100–280 nm, absorbed by ozone), UVB (280–315 nm, burning rays) and UVA (315–400 nm, ageing rays). Sunscreens are topical preparations containing UV filters that protect skin by reflecting/scattering or absorbing UV radiation.

2. Classification (draw Fig 3.4)

  1. Physical (inorganic) sunscreens: reflect and scatter UV: zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, kaolin, talc, calamine, iron oxides, magnesium oxide, red veterinary petrolatum.
  2. Chemical (organic) sunscreens: absorb UV and release heat:
    • UVB absorbers: PABA and esters (padimate O), cinnamates (octinoxate, cinoxate), salicylates (octisalate, homosalate), octocrylene, ensulizole.
    • UVA absorbers: benzophenones (oxybenzone, sulisobenzone), avobenzone, meradimate, ecamsule.
    • Broad-spectrum: bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole, drometrizole trisiloxane.
  3. By preparation type: sunscreen, sunblock, suntan, artificial tanning (DHA) and after-sun preparations.
  4. By dosage form: lotions, creams, gels, oils, sticks, sprays/aerosols, tinted make-up.

3. SPF

Sun Protection Factor is the ratio of the minimal erythemal dose (MED) on sunscreen-protected skin to the MED on unprotected skin, with the product applied at 2 mg/cm². SPF = MEDp / MEDu. % UVB blocked = (1 − 1/SPF) × 100 (SPF 15 → 93 %, SPF 30 → 97 %).

4. Method of determination

(a) In vivo (ISO 24444): fair-skinned volunteers (Fitzpatrick I–III); test sites marked on the back; product applied at 2 mg/cm² on one site, another left unprotected; after drying, sub-sites exposed to a graded series of UV doses from a xenon-arc solar simulator with a reference sunscreen alongside; erythema read 16–24 h later; the lowest dose giving perceptible redness = MED; SPF of each subject = MEDp/MEDu; mean SPF reported with 95 % confidence interval. (Draw Fig 3.6.)

(b) In vitro (Mansur): sample diluted in ethanol; absorbance read from 290 to 320 nm at 5 nm intervals; SPF = CF × Σ EE(λ) × I(λ) × Abs(λ), CF = 10, using standard EE × I values (290: 0.0150 · 295: 0.0817 · 300: 0.2874 · 305: 0.3278 · 310: 0.1864 · 315: 0.0839 · 320: 0.0180).

5. Role of sunscreens in preventing photo-ageing

  • Photo-ageing = premature ageing of sun-exposed skin: coarse wrinkles, leathery texture, laxity, age spots (lentigines), uneven pigmentation, telangiectasia.
  • Mechanism: UVA (and UVB) generate reactive oxygen species → activate collagen-degrading enzymes (matrix metalloproteinases) and reduce new collagen → solar elastosis and wrinkles.
  • Broad-spectrum sunscreens (UVB + UVA filters, ZnO/TiO₂) block this trigger; a long-term Australian trial (Nambour, 2013) found that daily sunscreen users showed no detectable increase in skin ageing over 4½ years.
  • Antioxidants (vitamins C, E, green tea) added to sunscreens neutralise free radicals; daily use, adequate quantity and reapplication are essential.

Conclusion

A combination of physical and chemical filters giving high SPF and good UVA protection, used daily and correctly, prevents sunburn, photo-ageing and skin cancer.

Q. Sun protection factor (SPF).
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS May 2022 · ~12 min
Definition + MED (1m) → formula + % blocked (1m) → methods: in vivo + Mansur (2m) → significance/limitations (1m)

Definition: SPF is the ratio of the minimal erythemal dose (MED) of UV on sunscreen-protected skin to the MED on unprotected skin, when the product is applied at 2 mg/cm². MED is the smallest UV dose that produces the first perceptible, clearly defined erythema 16–24 h after exposure.

SPF = MED (protected) ÷ MED (unprotected)

Example: if unprotected skin reddens in 10 min, an SPF 15 product theoretically delays it to 150 min. SPF 15 blocks ≈ 93 % and SPF 30 ≈ 97 % of UVB [(1 − 1/SPF) × 100].

Determination:

  1. In vivo (ISO 24444): volunteers (skin types I–III); product at 2 mg/cm² on the back; graded UV doses from a solar simulator to protected and unprotected sub-sites; MED read after 16–24 h; SPF = MEDp/MEDu (mean of ≥ 10 subjects).
  2. In vitro, Mansur method: absorbance of an ethanolic dilution read at 290–320 nm (every 5 nm); SPF = 10 × Σ EE(λ)·I(λ)·Abs(λ).
  3. In vitro, thin-film transmittance (Diffey–Robson; ISO 23675 double-plate method, 2024): product spread on a roughened PMMA plate/tape; UV transmittance measured and SPF calculated.

Significance: measures protection against sunburn (UVB); helps choose a product for skin type and exposure; allows comparison and label claims (low 6–10, medium 15–25, high 30–50, very high 50+). Limitations: does not measure UVA (needs UVA-PF/PA rating); real-life protection is lower because people apply less and sweat/water remove the film; reapply every 2 h.

Aloe barbadensis (Liliaceae/Asphodelaceae); inner gel with acemannan. Aloin (latex) is removed.
Turmeric: family and colour principle?
Zingiberaceae; curcuminoids (curcumin) ≈ 3–5 %.
Henna: family and dye?
Lythraceae; lawsone (2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone) binds keratin.
Amla: source and actives?
Emblica officinalis fruit (Euphorbiaceae); vitamin C + tannins (emblicanin A, B, gallic and ellagic acids).
Neem: family and dental actives?
Meliaceae; nimbidin, azadirachtin, nimbin → anti-plaque, gum care.
Clove: part used and active?
Dried flower buds (Myrtaceae); volatile oil ~15–20 % rich in eugenol (toothache, ZOE cement).

45 ± 1 °C, 60–70 % RH, 48 h → no oil/phase separation.

🎯 Quick Quiz

Which UV band is completely absorbed by the ozone layer?
UVC (100–280 nm) never reaches the ground; "C = Cancelled".
Zinc oxide protects mainly by…
It is a physical (inorganic) sunscreen: a mirror-like shield (it also absorbs some UV).
SPF is measured with the product applied at…
Standard application density 2 mg/cm²; people usually apply less, so real protection is lower.
The correction factor in Mansur's equation is…
SPF = 10 × Σ EE·I·Abs over 290–320 nm.
Lawsone, the colouring principle of henna, is chemically a…
Lawsone = 2-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone; aloin is the anthraquinone (in aloe latex).
Eugenol is the chief constituent of…
Clove buds contain ~15–20 % volatile oil, mostly eugenol → toothache relief.
Amla belongs to the family (textbook)…
Emblica officinalis: Euphorbiaceae (modern: Phyllanthaceae). Meliaceae = neem; Lythraceae = henna.
BIS minimum foam height for a 2 % shampoo solution is…
IS 7884: foam height ≥ 150 mm (Ross–Miles type, 30 °C). Don't confuse with toothpaste foaming power ≥ 50 mL.
The BIS standard for skin creams is…
6356 = toothpaste, 6608 = skin creams, 7884 = shampoo.
Maximum available fluoride in a fluoridated toothpaste (BIS / Cosmetics Rules 2020)?
IS 6356 Type 2 and Rule 38 of the Cosmetics Rules 2020: not more than 1000 ppm.

☀️ UV

UVC 100–280 (ozone) · UVB 280–315 (burn, epidermis, cancer, vit D) · UVA 315–400 (ageing, dermis, glass, tan). Textbook: 200–290 / 290–320 / 320–400.

🛡️ Classify sunscreens

Physical: ZnO, TiO₂, kaolin, talc, calamine. Chemical UVB: PABA, octinoxate, octisalate, homosalate, octocrylene, ensulizole. UVA: oxybenzone, avobenzone, meradimate, ecamsule. Broad: bemotrizinol, bisoctrizole. Types: sunscreen, sunblock, suntan, DHA tan, after-sun.

🔢 SPF

MEDp ÷ MEDu at 2 mg/cm²; MED read 16–24 h. In vivo ISO 24444 (volunteers, solar simulator). Mansur: 10 × Σ EE·I·Abs (290–320, 5 nm). % blocked = 1 − 1/SPF. EU: low 6–10, medium 15–25, high 30–50, very high 50+.
