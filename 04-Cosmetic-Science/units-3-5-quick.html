3.2 Sunscreens

📘 DefinitionSunscreens are cosmetic products that protect the skin from harmful effects of the sun (sunburn, suntan) by blocking UV radiation; also called sunblock / suntan lotion.

🪞 Physical (inorganic)

Titanium dioxide, zinc oxide, talc, kaolin, ferric chloride · reflect/scatter (large particles) · work immediately
VS

🧽 Chemical (organic)

PABA & esters, benzophenones, avobenzone, oxybenzone, octinoxate, octocrylene · absorb UV → heat · problem: photostability
🧠 Ideal sunscreen = "SAFE WATER"Stable (heat, light, sweat) · Absorbs 280–320 nm (UVB) · Free of odour/stain · Easily soluble · Water-resistant · Allows a little tan (300–400 nm) · Toxic-free, non-irritant · Effect lasts long · Remains stable through shelf-life

3.3 SPF: Sun Protection Factor

📘 Definition (⭐ asked)SPF measures how well a sunscreen protects against UV-B: how many times longer you can stay in the sun before the same burn, compared with no product.
SPF = MED (with sunscreen) ÷ MED (without sunscreen)MED = Minimal Erythemal Dose = least UV-B that causes the first visible redness
📘 Full-marks definition (write this one!)SPF = the UV energy needed to produce a minimal erythemal dose (MED) on protected skin ÷ the UV energy needed to produce an MED on unprotected skin. As SPF ↑, sunburn protection ↑.

📶 Classification by SPF value

🦡 Badger's SPF sunscreen testing

All sunscreens need FDA-approved SPF testing to make a UVB claim. 3 types: SPF static · water-resistant 40 min · water-resistant 80 min. All done in vivo (on human volunteers).

🪞 UNIT 4: Beauty Gadgets (Cosmetic Evaluation)

"Mirror, mirror on the wall, how oily, wet, leaky, coloured and strong are we all?"

4.1 Sebumeter: measures OIL 🛢️

📘 Principle (⭐ asked)Grease-spot photometry. A special matt tape (Sebufilm) touches the skin; sebum makes it transparent, like oil on paper. A photocell measures how much light passes → sebum in µg/cm².

Uses: classify skin as dry/normal/oily · test oil-control products & soaps · study acne/seborrhoea. Pros: quick, accurate, light. Cons: costly, needs calibration, sebum varies with age/weather.

4.2 Corneometer: measures WATER 💧

📘 Principle (⭐ asked)Capacitance. The probe is a tiny capacitor. Water has a very high dielectric constant (~81) compared with dry skin (<7), so more water in the stratum corneum = higher reading. Depth only 10–20 µm; result in arbitrary units (AU).

4.3 TEWL: water LEAKING out 🫗 (⭐⭐⭐)

Elsa's ice walls keep Arendelle's water inside. A healthy skin barrier does the same. When the wall cracks, water escapes: that's TEWL.

📘 DefinitionTrans-Epidermal Water Loss = amount of water passing from inside the body through the epidermis to the air (diffusion + evaporation), in g/h/m². Better barrier = lower TEWL.

① Open chamber: Tewameter

Open cylinder over the skin with 2 sensors (temperature + humidity) at two heights. They measure the water-vapour gradient (Fick's law) → evaporation rate.

② Closed chamber

a) Conductivity: evaporating water carries ions → conductivity rises.
b) Dew-point: silicon chip on a Peltier cooler detects the dew-point change as skin water arrives.

4.4 Skin Colour 🎨

Colour comes from melanin (brown), haemoglobin (red) and carotene (yellow). Inherited colour = constitutive; sun/age changes = acquired.

Instruments (non-invasive)

Colorimeter · Mexameter · reflectance spectrophotometer · chromameter · dermaspectrometer

How they work

Shine light → skin absorbs some → reflected light is read. Mexameter uses 3 wavelengths → melanin index + erythema (redness) index, scale 0–999.

4.5 Hair Tensile Strength 💪 (Rapunzel's rope test)

Flynn Rider climbed Rapunzel's hair and it didn't snap. That's tensile strength! 🗼

📘 DefinitionTensile strength = maximum stress (force per unit area) a hair can bear while being stretched before it breaks.
  • Pull a single hair (clamped at both ends) until it breaks; record force & stretch.
  • Stress = F / A; Strain = ΔL / L; plot them as the stress–strain curve.
  • Dry hair stretches 20–30%; wet hair up to ~50% (water makes keratin more elastic).
  • 3 test modes: CRE (constant rate of extension) · CRL (constant rate of load) · CRT (constant rate of traverse).
  • Also: SEM & AFM microscopy to see cuticle damage.
🧠 Unit 4 in 5 wordsOil (sebumeter) → Water (corneometer) → Leak (TEWL) → Colour (Mexameter) → Pull (tensile).

5.1 Oily vs Dry skin

🛢️ Oily skin

Too much sebum → shine, big pores, blackheads, pimples.
Causes: genetics, hot humid weather, over-washing, hormones (androgens: puberty, periods, pregnancy), medicines, poor diet, harsh cosmetics.
Care: gentle cleanser, oil-free products, remove make-up at night. Actives: salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, niacinamide, retinoids.
VS

🏜️ Dry skin (xerosis)

Too little water & lipids in the epidermis → rough, flaky, tight, itchy.
Care: moisturise right after bath, lukewarm water, humidifier, sunscreen, drink water, omega-3.

5.2 Skin moisturisation: 3 ways to keep water in

Ideal moisturiser: reduces TEWL · restores lipid barrier · hypoallergenic, fragrance-free · absorbs fast · cosmetically pleasant.

5.3 Hair & scalp: dandruff and hair fall

❄️

Dandruff = Olaf's snowfall on your shoulders

Shedding of dead scalp skin as white/yellow flakes + itch.
Causes: Malassezia fungus overgrowth ⭐, seborrhoeic dermatitis (oily, yellow flakes), dry scalp, poor hygiene, product sensitivity, stress, diet (zinc, B vitamins), hormones, weather.
Treat: shampoos with zinc pyrithione · selenium sulphide · ketoconazole · coal tar.
💇

Hair fall (alopecia)

Excess loss of hair from the scalp/body. Androgenetic alopecia = hereditary male-pattern baldness.
Causes "HHM-SSI-VL": Heredity · Hormones (pregnancy, menopause) · Medicines/radiation · Stress · Styling (heat, bleach) · harsh products · Infections · Vitamin/protein/iron deficiency · Lifestyle.
Treat: minoxidil, finasteride, ketoconazole shampoo.

5.4 Skin problems: the "spots & sweat" gang

🟤 Blemishes

Any mark, dark spot or discolouration. Types: acne marks, hyperpigmentation, scars, sun spots, freckles, birthmarks. Causes: excess oil, acne, sun, hormones, ageing. Treat: sunscreen, retinoids, vitamin C, peels, laser.

👵 Wrinkles (rhytides) ⭐

Lines/creases from ageing. Causes: less collagen & elastin, UV (photoageing), facial expressions, smoking, dehydration, genetics.
Types: dynamic (only when you smile/frown) · static (even at rest) · gravitational (sagging). Grandmother Willow has all three! 🌳

🔥 Prickly heat (miliaria) ⭐

Heat rash from blocked sweat ducts: sweat trapped under skin → red, itchy, prickly spots on chest, neck, armpits. Like Hades' hot head! Causes: hot humid weather, heavy sweating, tight synthetic clothes, friction. Treat: stay cool, loose cotton, calamine lotion, prickly-heat talc, avoid heavy creams.

🐗 Body odour (bromhidrosis)

Sweat itself has no smell! Bacteria break it into fatty acids & sulphur compounds → smell. Pumbaa knows. 😂
Eccrine glands (whole body, watery, odourless) vs apocrine glands (armpit, groin; protein-rich → bacteria love it → odour).

5.5 Antiperspirant vs Deodorant (⭐⭐⭐)

📘 Golden line"A deodorant is not an antiperspirant, but an antiperspirant can be a deodorant."

Actives

Aluminium chlorohydrate · aluminium chloride (liquids) · aluminium sulphate (creams) · basic Al bromide/nitrate · Mg-Al chloride

Other ingredients

Alcohol (quick-dry, cool) · antimicrobials · fragrance · carriers (hydrogenated castor oil, stearyl alcohol) · parabens · propellants (butane, propane)

Forms

Roll-on (liquid) · stick · aerosol spray

Ideal: pleasant odour, spreads easily, quick-drying, non-staining, non-irritant, long-lasting.
