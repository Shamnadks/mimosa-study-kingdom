1 Oily & Dry Skin: Know the Skin Type First

Every Beauty Lab consultation starts with a skin-type card, because the right product depends on it.

🎬 Story mode

Two customers walk in. Elsa has spent days in her icy palace; her cheeks feel tight and look flaky after the frosty wind. That's dry skin. Moana has been paddling under the tropical sun; by noon her forehead and nose shine like the sea, but her cheeks are fine. That's a combination skin with an oily T-zone. The Fairy Godmother doesn't give them the same cream. She first checks oil (Sebumeter), water (Corneometer) and barrier (TEWL), just as you learnt in Unit 4.

📘 Exam definition

Skin type is the basic, mostly genetically decided character of a person's skin, determined mainly by its sebum output and its water-holding (hydration) capacity and sensitivity. Skin types are classified as normal, dry, oily, combination and sensitive.
Oily skin (seborrhoea) produces excess sebum, giving a shiny, greasy surface with enlarged pores and a tendency to comedones and acne. Dry skin (xerosis) has too little sebum and/or water in the stratum corneum, so it feels rough, tight and flaky.

🛢️ Why is skin oily?

  • Androgens (puberty, PCOS) enlarge and stimulate sebaceous glands
  • Genetics (large glands run in families)
  • Hot, humid climate like Kerala's
  • Stress (hormonal effects), some drugs (steroids)
  • Heavy, comedogenic creams and hair oils on the face

🧴 Care for oily skin

  • Gentle foaming cleanser twice a day (salicylic acid / syndet)
  • Oil-free, non-comedogenic gel moisturiser (glycerin, hyaluronic acid)
  • Oil absorbers: kaolin/bentonite clay masks, mattifying powders
  • Sebum-regulating actives: niacinamide, zinc PCA
  • Gel sunscreen; avoid over-scrubbing

For "classify skin types" (2 marks), write the 5 types with one feature each. For anything on dry skin, open with the skin-type definition and one line on how it is measured (Sebumeter, Corneometer, TEWL). That bridges Units 4 and 5 nicely.

2 Causes Leading to Dry Skin: Elsa's Endless Winter

Three KUHS short notes in a row (May 22, Jul 23, Dec 23). This section is pure gold.

🎬 Story mode

When Elsa's powers freeze Arendelle, the air becomes cold and dry and the wind howls. Her skin's water evaporates faster (↑ TEWL). She scrubs her hands with harsh soap and hot water to warm up, and that strips the protective lipids. The top skin layer runs short of its own water magnets (NMF), the "mortar" cracks, flakes appear, it itches, she scratches, and the barrier breaks even more. Dry skin is a vicious cycle, and every cause you list simply pushes that cycle round.

📘 Exam definition (write exactly this)

Dry skin (xerosis) is a condition in which the water content of the stratum corneum falls below normal (healthy SC holds roughly 10–20 % water; below about 10 % it loses flexibility). It results from an impaired barrier (loss of intercellular lipids), reduced natural moisturising factor (NMF) and/or reduced sebum. The skin is rough, scaly, tight, dull and itchy, with fine lines and sometimes cracks (fissures).

⚠️ Common mistake

Dry skin ≠ dehydrated skin. Dry skin is a skin type or condition with too little lipid/NMF. "Dehydrated" skin lacks water temporarily and can even be oily. Also, dry skin is not caused by drinking too little water alone. Write the barrier/NMF mechanism.

3 Skin Moisturisation: The Three Spells

How skin keeps itself moist, and how moisturisers help by sealing, pulling and smoothing.

🎬 Story mode

The Fairy Godmother has three spells for Elsa's thirsty skin. Spell 1, "Seal!": a thin, glassy ice lid over the skin so water can't escape (occlusives). Spell 2, "Pull!": tiny water magnets that draw moisture up into the top layer (humectants). Spell 3, "Smooth!": soft oils that fill the gaps between lifted flakes so the skin feels like silk (emollients). The best moisturiser casts all three at once.

📘 Exam definition

Skin moisturisation is the process of increasing and maintaining the water content of the stratum corneum and restoring its barrier, so the skin stays soft, smooth and flexible. A moisturiser is a cosmetic that does this by occlusion, humectancy, emolliency and barrier-lipid replacement.

How healthy skin keeps itself moist

💧 Natural Moisturising Factor (NMF)

A mix of small, water-soluble, hygroscopic substances inside the corneocytes, formed mainly when the protein filaggrin breaks down into amino acids. NMF holds water within the cells and keeps the SC flexible. Soap and water wash it out.

🧱 Intercellular lipid "mortar"

Ordered lamellae of ceramides, cholesterol and free fatty acids (in roughly equal molar amounts) between the corneocyte "bricks". They block water loss (Fig 4.3, Unit 4).

🛢️ Sebum film / acid mantle

The surface lipid film and slightly acidic pH (~4.5–5.5) support the barrier and the skin flora.

🔁 Water supply from below

Water diffuses up from the hydrated dermis. Tight junctions in the living epidermis also help hold it.

💊 Pharmacy example

In atopic dermatitis, emollient therapy is first-line: the pharmacist counsels "soak and seal" (apply within minutes of bathing), generous amounts, twice daily. For cracked heels, a urea cream works as humectant plus keratolytic. Both claims are proven with the Corneometer and TEWL (Unit 4).

4 Comedogenic & Dermatitis: Two Must-Know Terms

These two words are in the PCI syllabus text for Unit V ("basic understanding of the terms"). They are easy 2-markers in question banks.

🚫 Comedogenic

Comedogenic describes an ingredient or product that tends to block the pilosebaceous follicles and cause comedones (blackheads and whiteheads). Non-comedogenic products are formulated and tested not to do this.

  • Commonly comedogenic: isopropyl myristate, acetylated lanolin and lanolin derivatives, cocoa butter, coconut oil, wheat-germ oil, some heavy waxes, certain D&C red pigments.
  • Acne cosmetica / pomade acne: acne caused by cosmetics or hair oils (forehead acne from hair oil is very common in India!).
  • Testing: historically the rabbit-ear assay; today human back tests in which follicular casts (cyanoacrylate biopsy) are counted.
  • Acnegenic (causing inflamed papules) is a related but different term.

🔥 Dermatitis

Dermatitis (eczema) means inflammation of the skin, with redness, itching, swelling, vesicles, oozing and later scaling and thickening.

  • Irritant contact dermatitis (ICD): direct non-immune damage from detergents, solvents, acids/alkalis, wet work. The most common type.
  • Allergic contact dermatitis (ACD): type IV (delayed, T-cell) hypersensitivity. Cosmetic allergens: PPD in hair dyes, fragrances, preservatives (methylisothiazolinone, formaldehyde releasers), nickel, lanolin. Diagnosed by patch test.
  • Atopic dermatitis: genetic barrier defect (filaggrin) plus allergy; dry, itchy skin.
  • Seborrhoeic dermatitis: oily areas, Malassezia; dandruff is its mildest form.
  • Photocontact dermatitis: chemical plus UV. Also napkin (diaper) and stasis dermatitis.
💊 Pharmacy example

Every PPD hair-dye pack tells the user to do a skin (allergy) test 48 hours before dyeing. That's to catch allergic contact dermatitis before it happens. Cosmetic companies also run human repeat-insult patch tests (HRIPT) to prove "dermatologically tested / hypoallergenic" claims.

⭐ Exam tip

Management of dermatitis in 4 points: identify and avoid the cause (patch test) → emollients and barrier creams → mild syndet cleansers → topical corticosteroid/calamine for symptoms (medical).

5 Dandruff: Olaf's Snowflakes

White flakes on dark shoulders: the most common scalp complaint, and the reason anti-dandruff shampoos sell so well.

🎬 Story mode

Olaf has his own little snow flurry following him everywhere, and flakes land on everyone's shoulders. On a human scalp, the "flurry" comes from a tiny yeast called Malassezia that lives on everyone's scalp and loves to eat sebum. While feeding, it leaves behind irritating fatty acids. In people whose scalp is sensitive to them, the skin speeds up cell turnover to defend itself, and big clumps of cells fall off as visible flakes. Fix the yeast, the oil or the speed of turnover, and the flurry stops.

📘 Exam definition

Dandruff (pityriasis capitis) is a common chronic scalp condition marked by excessive, visible shedding of white or greyish scales from the scalp, often with itching and little or no inflammation. It is the mildest form of seborrhoeic dermatitis and is associated with the yeast Malassezia (mainly M. globosa and M. restricta).

⛄ Causes and aggravating factors

  • Oily scalp, puberty and androgens (more common in young men)
  • Infrequent shampooing, so oil and cells build up
  • Cold, dry winter; hot humid sweating
  • Stress, fatigue, illness (immune changes)
  • Poor diet (zinc, B-vitamin, fatty-acid deficiency)
  • Sensitivity to hair products (contact dermatitis); heavy oiling may feed Malassezia
  • Conditions such as Parkinson's disease and HIV (worse seborrhoeic dermatitis)

🔎 Types

  • Dry dandruff (pityriasis simplex capitis): fine, dry, white, powdery flakes.
  • Oily/waxy dandruff (pityriasis steatoides): greasy, yellowish, sticky scales with itching.
  • Don't confuse it with dry scalp (small flakes from dryness, no Malassezia role) or scalp psoriasis (thick silvery plaques).

6 Hair Fall: Causes, Types & Prevention

Losing some hair every day is normal. Losing more than you grow is alopecia.

🎬 Story mode

Remember how Rapunzel's magic hair lost its glow (and its golden colour) when it was cut? Real hair isn't magic. Each strand lives on a timetable: grow → pause → rest → fall, then a new one starts. Hair fall becomes a problem when too many strands jump to the "rest-and-fall" stage at once (after illness or stress), when the growing stage gets shorter and the hair thinner (hormones and genes), when the body's own guards attack the roots (autoimmune), or when tight braids pull them out (traction).

📘 Exam definition

Hair fall is the shedding of hair from the scalp. Shedding about 50–100 hairs a day is normal. Alopecia is abnormal, partial or complete loss of hair from areas where it normally grows, temporary or permanent, localised or diffuse.

7 Blemishes: Spots on the Glass Slipper

Any mark that spoils an even complexion: pimples, dark spots, freckles, scars.

🎬 Story mode

Cinderella's glass slipper is perfect, but imagine tiny smudges on it: a fingerprint here, a dust spot there. The slipper is still beautiful, yet the eye goes straight to the marks. Skin blemishes are the same: small spots or patches of a different colour or texture. Most come from too much melanin in one place (after sun, hormones or a squeezed pimple), from clogged pores, or from scars.

📘 Exam definition

A blemish is any mark, spot, discolouration or flaw on the skin that spoils its even appearance, for example acne lesions and marks, dark spots (hyperpigmentation), freckles, age spots, melasma, scars and moles. Most cosmetic blemishes are harmless but affect appearance and confidence.

8 Wrinkles: Grandmother Willow's Wisdom Lines

Two KUHS short notes so far (May 22 with prickly heat, Nov 22 "types and reasons").

🎬 Story mode

Grandmother Willow, the wise old tree in Pocahontas, has a bark-face full of lines. Some appear only when she smiles or frowns (dynamic, expression lines). Some stay even when she rests (static lines). Some are fine little crinkles in thin, papery bark (atrophic). Some are deep, leathery furrows on the side that faced the sun for centuries (elastotic, photo-ageing). And her heavy branches droop into folds (gravitational). Every type has its own reason, and that's exactly what KUHS asks.

📘 Exam definition

Wrinkles (rhytides) are creases, folds or ridges on the skin surface. They form as the skin ages and the dermis loses collagen, elastin and hyaluronic acid, the epidermis thins, fat pads shrink and facial muscles fold the skin repeatedly. The process is greatly accelerated by sun exposure (photo-ageing) and smoking.

Types of wrinkles

A. By depth

  • Fine lines: shallow, superficial (epidermis/upper dermis); vanish when skin is stretched; improved by moisturisers.
  • Coarse / deep wrinkles (furrows): deep creases in the dermis; persist when stretched.

B. By behaviour

  • Dynamic (expression) wrinkles: appear only with muscle movement (smiling, frowning, squinting); forehead, frown lines, crow's feet.
  • Static wrinkles: visible at rest; caused by loss of collagen and elasticity and by gravity. Dynamic lines become static with age.

12 Antiperspirants & Deodorants: Actives and Mechanism

Asked in EVERY KUHS paper and the model paper. Learn it like your name.

🎬 Story mode

The Fairy Godmother gives Pumbaa two magic bottles. Bottle 1, the antiperspirant, puts a tiny cork (plug) in the top of each sweat duct, so less sweat reaches the skin and the bacteria go hungry. Bottle 2, the deodorant, lets him sweat, but Kills the bacteria, Blocks their smell-making enzymes, Soaks up any smell that forms, and Cloaks the rest with a lovely perfume. Corks vs Kill-Block-Soak-Cloak!

📘 Exam definitions

Antiperspirants are preparations that reduce the amount of sweat (perspiration) reaching the skin surface, mainly the axillae, by applying astringent aluminium or aluminium–zirconium salts that temporarily plug the eccrine sweat ducts. Because there is less sweat for bacteria, they also reduce odour. (In the USA they are OTC drugs that must reduce sweating by at least 20 %, or 30 % for an "extra effective" claim; in India and the EU they are cosmetics.)
Deodorants are preparations that prevent, reduce or mask body odour without stopping sweating. They work by antimicrobial action on odour-forming bacteria, by inhibiting bacterial enzymes, by absorbing or neutralising odour molecules, and by masking with fragrance.

🧴 Antiperspirant

  • Target: the sweat (eccrine ducts)
  • Actives: Al / Al–Zr salts
  • Mechanism: plug in the sweat duct + astringent
  • Effect: ↓ wetness and ↓ odour
  • Regulatory: an OTC drug in the USA
VS

🌸 Deodorant

  • Target: bacteria and odour
  • Actives: antimicrobials, odour absorbers, fragrance
  • Mechanism: Kill · Block · Soak · Cloak
  • Effect: ↓ odour only (sweat continues)
  • Regulatory: a cosmetic

  1. Plug (obstruction) theory (main, accepted): the dissolved aluminium salt diffuses into the upper part of the eccrine sweat duct. There the higher pH makes the aluminium species hydrolyse and polymerise into an insoluble aluminium hydroxide gel, which binds with mucopolysaccharides and proteins of the duct wall to form a superficial plug. Sweat is still made but cannot reach the surface. The plug is temporary and is lost with normal epidermal renewal, so regular reapplication is needed.
  2. Astringent action: aluminium ions precipitate surface proteins and cause swelling and constriction of the duct opening.
  3. Leaky-duct / permeability theory: the salt makes the duct wall leaky so sweat is reabsorbed into surrounding tissue before reaching the surface.
  4. Antibacterial (secondary deodorant) effect: aluminium salts are acidic and mildly antibacterial, and less moisture means fewer bacteria. So an antiperspirant also deodorises.

Efficacy depends on: the salt (Al–Zr > ACH > milder salts), concentration, polymer size ("activated" salts), vehicle, applying to dry skin at night (glands least active, so the plug forms best), and regular use. Testing: gravimetric "hot-room" sweat collection (absorbent pads weighed, treated vs untreated axilla); deodorancy by trained odour judges ("sniff test").

Q. Structure of skin and causes for dry skin.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS May 2022 · ~12 min · 1½ pages
Skin intro + labelled diagram (1½m) → 3 layers with sub-layers (1m) → Dry skin definition (½m) → Classified causes (2m)

A. Structure of skin

The skin is the largest organ of the body (about 1.5–2 m² in adults). It protects, regulates temperature, senses and synthesises vitamin D. It has three layers:

1. Epidermis: keratinised stratified squamous epithelium without blood vessels. From outside in: stratum corneum (flat dead corneocytes in a lipid matrix, the barrier) → stratum lucidum (palms and soles only) → stratum granulosum (keratohyalin and lamellar granules) → stratum spinosum (prickle cells, Langerhans cells) → stratum basale (dividing keratinocytes, melanocytes, Merkel cells). Cells renew in about 4 weeks. Mnemonic: "Come Let's Get Sun Burnt."

2. Dermis: connective tissue of collagen (strength), elastin (recoil) and ground substance (hyaluronic acid), made by fibroblasts. It has a papillary layer (loose, capillaries) and a reticular layer (dense), and contains blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, sebaceous glands, sweat glands and arrector pili.

3. Hypodermis (subcutis): adipose tissue for insulation, cushioning and energy storage.

B. Dry skin and its causes

Dry skin (xerosis): the water content of the stratum corneum falls below normal (below ~10 %) because of barrier-lipid loss, reduced NMF or reduced sebum. The skin becomes rough, scaly, tight and itchy.

  • Environmental: low humidity, cold windy winter, heaters/AC, UV.
  • Washing habits: hot water, frequent bathing, alkaline soaps and detergents (strip lipids, wash out NMF).
  • Occupational/chemical: wet work, solvents, sanitisers.
  • Age: ↓ sebum, NMF and lipids in the elderly; menopause.
  • Genetic/disease: ichthyosis (filaggrin defect), atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, hypothyroidism, diabetes, renal failure.
  • Drugs: retinoids, diuretics, statins, benzoyl peroxide.
  • Nutritional: lack of essential fatty acids, vitamin A, zinc; poor water intake.

Conclusion: an intact stratum corneum barrier keeps skin hydrated. Dry skin results when its lipids, NMF or sebum are lost, and it is corrected with moisturisers (occlusives, humectants, emollients).

Q. Explain dry skin. What are the causes of dry skin? / Explain in detail the causes leading to dry skin.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 & Jul 2023 · ~12 min
Definition + signs (1m) → Mechanism / vicious cycle diagram (1m) → Classified causes table (2½m) → Remedies + conclusion (½m)

Definition: Dry skin (xerosis) is a condition in which the stratum corneum contains less water than normal (healthy SC ≈ 10–20 %, dry below ≈ 10 %) because of impaired barrier lipids, reduced natural moisturising factor (NMF) and/or reduced sebum.

Signs: roughness, scaling/flaking, tightness after washing, itching, dull appearance, fine lines, cracks and fissures (heels, hands), redness.

Mechanism (draw Fig 5.2): barrier damage → ↑ TEWL → ↓ SC water and NMF → desquamation enzymes fail → scales and cracks → itch and scratching → further barrier damage (vicious cycle).

Causes:

  1. Environmental: cold, dry, windy weather; low humidity; indoor heating and air-conditioning; excessive sun (UV damages lipids).
  2. Excessive washing: hot water, long baths, alkaline soaps, harsh detergents (SLS), scrubbing, chlorinated pools, alcohol toners. These remove sebum, intercellular lipids and NMF.
  3. Occupational: wet work (nurses, cooks, cleaners), solvents (acetone, kerosene), cement, frequent sanitiser use.
  4. Ageing: reduced sebum, NMF, ceramide synthesis and cell renewal; ↓ oestrogen after menopause.
  5. Genetic: naturally dry skin type, ichthyosis vulgaris (filaggrin mutation → less NMF), atopic predisposition.
  6. Skin diseases: atopic and contact dermatitis, psoriasis.
  7. Systemic diseases: hypothyroidism, diabetes, chronic kidney and liver disease, malnutrition, HIV.
  8. Drugs: isotretinoin, topical retinoids, diuretics, statins, benzoyl peroxide, chemotherapy.
  9. Nutritional: deficiency of essential fatty acids, vitamin A, zinc, niacin; dehydration.
  10. Lifestyle: smoking, stress, rough woollen clothing, friction.

Remedies: lukewarm short baths, mild syndet cleansers, moisturiser on damp skin ("soak and seal") with occlusives + humectants + ceramides, urea or lactic acid creams for scaling, humidifiers, gloves, sunscreen, adequate water and EFAs.

Conclusion: dry skin is a barrier problem. Removing the cause and restoring lipids and water with moisturisers breaks the vicious cycle.

Q. Explain body odour. Explain the mechanism of antiperspirants.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS May 2022 · ~12 min
Body odour: definition, glands, bacterial mechanism, causes (2½m) → Antiperspirant: definition, actives, plug mechanism diagram (2½m)

A. Body odour

Body odour is the unpleasant smell formed when skin bacteria break down odourless sweat, sebum and skin debris into volatile compounds. Excessive offensive odour is called bromhidrosis.

  • Eccrine glands (whole body, open on the skin) secrete watery sweat for cooling. It is odourless, but the moisture supports bacterial growth.
  • Apocrine glands (axilla, groin, areola; open into hair follicles; active from puberty) secrete a lipid- and protein-rich fluid. This is the main source of axillary odour.
  • Bacteria (Corynebacterium spp., Staphylococcus hominis, S. epidermidis, Micrococcus) break the precursors down into volatile fatty acids (e.g. 3-methyl-2-hexenoic acid), thioalcohols and 16-androstenes, which give the odour (draw Fig 5.8).
  • Aggravating factors: hyperhidrosis, heat and humidity, poor hygiene, hairy axillae, synthetic clothing, obesity, diet (garlic, onion, spices), stress, diseases such as diabetes and trimethylaminuria.

B. Mechanism of antiperspirants

Antiperspirants reduce the sweat reaching the skin surface. Actives: aluminium chloride, aluminium chlorohydrate (ACH), aluminium sesquichlorohydrate, aluminium zirconium tetrachlorohydrex gly.

  1. Plug formation (main mechanism): the aluminium salt dissolves in sweat and diffuses into the eccrine duct opening. At the higher pH inside the duct it hydrolyses and polymerises into an aluminium hydroxide gel, which combines with duct mucopolysaccharides and proteins to form a plug that blocks sweat outflow (draw Fig 5.9). The plug is temporary and sheds with epidermal renewal, so daily use is needed.
  2. Astringent effect: it precipitates proteins and constricts the duct opening.
  3. Altered duct permeability: sweat is reabsorbed through a leaky duct wall.
  4. Antibacterial: acidic Al salts plus less moisture mean fewer bacteria, so odour falls too.

Best applied to dry skin at night. In the USA an antiperspirant must reduce sweating by at least 20 %.

Q. What are deodorants? Explain their mechanism.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · ~12 min
Definition + body-odour basis (1m) → Mechanisms 1–5 with actives (3m) → Forms + one formula (1m)

Definition: Deodorants are cosmetic preparations applied mainly to the axillae (and feet) that prevent, reduce or mask body odour without significantly reducing the amount of sweat.

Basis: fresh sweat is odourless. Odour forms when skin bacteria (Corynebacterium, Staphylococcus) degrade apocrine secretion into volatile fatty acids, thioalcohols and androstenes.

Mechanisms of deodorants

  1. Antimicrobial action: reduces the odour-causing flora. Ethanol, quaternary ammonium compounds (benzethonium chloride, cetylpyridinium chloride), chlorhexidine, polyaminopropyl biguanide, farnesol, zinc salts, silver citrate, essential oils (triclosan was widely used but is now restricted).
  2. Enzyme inhibition: prevents bacterial enzymes from releasing odour from precursors. Triethyl citrate (esterase inhibitor; liberated citric acid lowers pH), zinc glycinate.
  3. Odour absorption / chemical neutralisation: zinc ricinoleate binds thiols and amines; cyclodextrins encapsulate odour; sodium bicarbonate neutralises fatty acids; activated charcoal and zeolites adsorb.
  4. Odour masking: fragrances and essential oils cover residual odour.
  5. Antioxidant action: BHT and tocopherol stop unsaturated fatty acids turning rancid.
  6. Often combined with antiperspirant salts (ACH) to reduce moisture.

Forms: sprays, roll-ons, sticks, powders, creams, wipes. Example (deodorant stick): propylene glycol 70 %, sodium stearate 7 %, antimicrobial 0.5 %, fragrance 1.5 %, water to 100 %.

Q. Prickly heat and wrinkles.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS May 2022 · ~12 min
Prickly heat: definition, types table, causes, treatment (2½m) → Wrinkles: definition, types, causes, treatment (2½m)

A. Prickly heat (miliaria)

A skin eruption due to blockage of eccrine sweat ducts, so sweat leaks into the skin, causing tiny vesicles or itchy, prickling red papules in hot, humid weather.

  • Miliaria crystallina: block at the stratum corneum; clear, superficial, non-itchy vesicles.
  • Miliaria rubra (true prickly heat): block in the epidermis; red, itchy, stinging papules (most common).
  • Miliaria pustulosa: rubra with pustules.
  • Miliaria profunda: leakage into the dermis; flesh-coloured papules; reduced sweating.

Causes: heat, humidity, heavy sweating, tight synthetic clothes, occlusive creams, immature ducts in infants, fever, bed-rest. Treatment: cool environment, loose cotton clothes, cool baths, calamine lotion, prickly heat powders (talc, zinc oxide, kaolin, menthol, camphor), avoid heavy creams; mild steroid if severe. Diagram: Fig 5.7.

B. Wrinkles

Creases or folds of skin from ageing loss of collagen, elastin and hyaluronic acid, accelerated by UV.

  • Types: fine vs deep; dynamic (expression) vs static; Piérard types: atrophic, elastotic (solar), expressional, gravitational folds. Sites: forehead, glabellar, crow's feet, nasolabial, marionette, perioral.
  • Causes: intrinsic ageing (↓ collagen/elastin/HA, thin skin, ↓ oestrogen), photo-ageing (UVA → ROS → MMPs), smoking, pollution, repeated expressions, dry skin, weight loss, glycation.
  • Treatment: sunscreen, retinoids, AHAs, peptides, antioxidants (vitamin C, E, niacinamide), hyaluronic acid moisturisers; botulinum toxin and fillers (medical).
Q. What are the different types of wrinkles? Give reasons.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · ~12 min
Definition (½m) → Face diagram of sites (1m) → Types: depth, behaviour, Piérard 4 types table (2m) → Reasons: intrinsic vs extrinsic (1½m)

Definition: Wrinkles (rhytides) are lines, creases or folds on the skin that develop as the dermis loses collagen, elastin and hyaluronic acid, the epidermis thins and the skin is repeatedly folded by muscles and pulled by gravity.

Diagram: face with numbered sites (Fig 5.5): forehead lines, glabellar "11" lines, crow's feet, nasolabial folds, marionette lines, perioral lines.

Types

  1. Fine lines: shallow, superficial, disappear on stretching (often from dryness).
  2. Coarse / deep wrinkles: deep dermal furrows that remain on stretching.
  3. Dynamic (expression) wrinkles: appear with facial movement (frown, smile, squint); forehead, glabella, crow's feet.
  4. Static wrinkles: present at rest due to loss of elasticity and gravity.
  5. Atrophic wrinkles: fine parallel crinkles in thin elderly skin.
  6. Elastotic (solar) wrinkles: deep, criss-cross, permanent creases on sun-exposed skin (solar elastosis).
  7. Gravitational folds: nasolabial folds, marionette lines, jowls from sagging.

Reasons

  • Intrinsic ageing: ↓ collagen and elastin synthesis, ↓ hyaluronic acid (less dermal water), thinning of epidermis and dermis, flattening of the dermo-epidermal junction, ↓ sebum, ↓ oestrogen at menopause, loss of fat pads, genetics.
  • Photo-ageing (UV): UVA penetrates the dermis and generates free radicals that activate matrix metalloproteinases, which degrade collagen; abnormal elastin accumulates (elastosis).
  • Smoking (oxidative stress, ↓ blood supply), pollution, repeated expressions, sleeping posture, dry skin, rapid weight loss, glycation from a high-sugar diet, stress and poor sleep.

Conclusion: sunscreen and avoiding smoking prevent most extrinsic wrinkles. Retinoids, peptides and antioxidants improve existing ones.

Q. Explain hair fall. Explain in detail its prevention.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · ~12 min
Definition + normal loss (½m) → Hair cycle sketch (1m) → Types/causes (1½m) → Prevention in detail (2m)

Hair fall is the shedding of hairs from the scalp. Losing 50–100 hairs a day is normal, because hairs in the telogen phase are replaced. Excessive loss leading to thinning or bald areas is alopecia.

Hair cycle (Fig 5.4): anagen (growth, 2–7 years, ~85–90 % of hairs) → catagen (regression, 2–3 weeks) → telogen (rest, ~3 months) → exogen (shedding).

Types and causes: androgenetic alopecia (genes + DHT, follicle miniaturisation); telogen effluvium (fever, childbirth, crash diet, stress, thyroid, iron deficiency: diffuse shedding 2–3 months later); anagen effluvium (chemotherapy); alopecia areata (autoimmune patches); traction alopecia (tight hairstyles); breakage from bleaching, perming, heat; dandruff and scalp infections; drugs.

Prevention

  1. Nutrition: balanced diet with enough protein, iron, zinc, biotin, vitamin D and B12; correct anaemia; avoid crash dieting.
  2. Treat underlying causes: thyroid disorders, PCOS, infections, dandruff (anti-dandruff shampoo); review drugs.
  3. Gentle hair care: mild shampoo plus conditioner (reduces combing friction and breakage); wide-toothed comb; detangle wet hair gently; avoid rubbing with a towel.
  4. Avoid damage: loose hairstyles (no tight ponytails or braids), limited heat styling with heat protectants, fewer chemical treatments (bleach, perm, straightening), protection from sun and chlorine.
  5. Lifestyle: stress management, adequate sleep, regular scalp massage, no smoking.
  6. Cosmetic and medical actives: caffeine or peptide hair tonics; herbal oils (amla, bhringraj, hibiscus) as supportive care; minoxidil topical (prolongs anagen); finasteride (men, prescription); steroid injections for alopecia areata.

Normal 50–100/day. Androgenetic (DHT), telogen effluvium (2–3 months post-trigger), anagen effluvium (chemo), areata (autoimmune), traction. Causes 4H + 4D. Minoxidil, finasteride, gentle care, nutrition.

🟤 Blemishes · 🌳 Wrinkles

Blemishes: acne marks/PIH, freckles, lentigines, melasma, scars → sunscreen, kojic acid, niacinamide, vitamin C, AHAs. Wrinkles: fine/deep, dynamic/static, atrophic/elastotic/expressional/gravitational; ageing + UV (MMPs) + smoking → retinol, peptides, antioxidants.

🔴 Acne · 🔥 Miliaria

Acne: Oil, Plug, Bug (C. acnes), Fire → BPO, salicylic acid, sulphur, azelaic acid. Miliaria: duct block. Crystallina (SC, clear) · rubra (epidermis, red, itchy) · profunda (dermis) → cool, cotton, calamine, powder.

🐗 BO · 🧴 AP/Deo

Apocrine sweat + Corynebacterium/Staph. → VFAs, thioalcohols, androstenes. AP: ACH (≤25 %), AlCl₃ (≤15 %), AZG (≤20 %) → Al(OH)₃ plug. Deo: Kill (alcohol, quats), Block (triethyl citrate), Soak (zinc ricinoleate), Cloak (fragrance).
