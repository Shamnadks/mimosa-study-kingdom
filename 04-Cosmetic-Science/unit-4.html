1 Principles of Cosmetic Evaluation

Why do we measure at all, what kinds of tests exist, and what rules make a measurement trustworthy?

🎬 Story mode

The Fairy Godmother proudly labels her new cream: "Makes skin 30 % more hydrated!" The Royal Inspector raises one eyebrow: "Prove it." She can't just say "Bibbidi-Bobbidi-Boo". She has to bring volunteers into a calm, cool room, measure their skin before the cream and after it with a proper instrument, compare against an untreated patch, and show the numbers. That careful, fair, numbers-first approach is cosmetic evaluation.

📘 Exam definition (write exactly this)

Cosmetic evaluation is the systematic testing of a cosmetic product, or of the skin and hair treated with it, for quality, safety and efficacy using sensory, clinical and instrumental methods, so that the claims made on the label can be substantiated.

Skin bioengineering means non-invasive, instrument-based measurement of skin properties (sebum, hydration, barrier, colour, elasticity) on living people.

Why evaluate a cosmetic?

📣

Claim substantiation

"Moisturises 24 h" or "reduces oiliness" has to be backed by data. Under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act, a cosmetic whose label carries a false or misleading statement is misbranded.
🛡️

Safety

Detect irritation (redness, barrier damage) before the product reaches the market.
⚖️

Efficacy and comparison

Choose the best prototype, or show that the product beats its vehicle or a competitor.
🏭

Quality

Batch-to-batch consistency, and stability of performance over time.
🔬

Research

Understand skin types, ageing, the effect of season and so on.
😊

Consumer acceptance

Sensory feel, spreadability, fragrance, and how much people like the product.

Principles every good measurement follows

🧘 Standardised conditions

  • Volunteers acclimatise (rest) for about 15–30 min before measuring.
  • A controlled room: steady temperature (around 20–22 °C) and steady humidity, with no drafts or direct sun.
  • The same site (e.g. volar forearm, forehead) and the same time of day.
  • No washing or products on the test site for a fixed time beforehand.

📏 Good study design

  • Baseline reading, then readings at fixed time points.
  • An untreated or vehicle control site, randomised and ideally blinded.
  • Repeat readings, enough volunteers, and statistics.
  • Calibrated instruments and published guidelines, such as those of EEMCO (European Expert Group on Efficacy Measurement of Cosmetics and other topical products).

2 Sebumeter: Measuring the Oil

How oily is this forehead? The sebumeter answers in micrograms per square centimetre.

🎬 Story mode

Cinderella is getting ready for the ball and her nose is shiny. The Fairy Godmother presses a little frosted ribbon on it for a few seconds. Wherever there was oil, the frosted ribbon turns see-through, like a butter spot on brown paper. Then she holds the ribbon up to a lamp: "The more light that passes through, the more oil you had, dear." That butter-spot trick is exactly how the sebumeter works.

📘 Exam definition

The Sebumeter is a non-invasive instrument that measures the amount of sebum (casual sebum level) on the surface of skin, scalp or hair by grease-spot photometry. A special opaque (matt) plastic tape becomes transparent in proportion to the sebum it absorbs. Its light transmission is then measured by a photocell and shown as µg sebum/cm².

First, what is sebum?

Sebum is the oily secretion of the sebaceous glands, which open into hair follicles and are most numerous on the face (the T-zone), scalp, chest and upper back. Roughly, it is made of triglycerides + free fatty acids (~57 %), wax esters (~25 %), squalene (~12 %), and cholesterol and its esters (~4 %). It lubricates skin and hair and forms part of the surface film. Too much gives oily skin, shine and acne; too little gives dry skin.

Construction (what to label)

🎞️ Measuring cassette (probe)

  • Holds a roll of special matt synthetic tape (about 0.1 mm thick).
  • A window exposes 64 mm² of tape to the skin.
  • A spring presses the tape on the skin with constant pressure, so readings can be repeated.
  • A trigger advances fresh tape; used tape rewinds inside. Hygienic, and each cassette lasts about 400–450 readings.

💡 Main unit (photometer)

  • An aperture/slot into which the cassette is inserted.
  • A light source on one side of the tape and a photocell on the other.
  • A microprocessor converts transmitted light into sebum content.
  • A display shows µg/cm².

Procedure

🧘Acclimatise volunteerno washing or products on the site
0️⃣Zero-calibratereading on unused tape
👇Press cassette on skinfixed time, ~30 s
🔦Insert into aperturetransmission measured
📟Read µg/cm²advance the tape
🧠 Mnemonic: "BUTTER SPOT"

Butter on brown paper makes a see-through spot. Sebum on matt tape → transparent → light passes → photocell → µg/cm². Say it as: "Tape, Touch, Transparent, Transmit, Tell."

TapeTouchTransparentTransmitTell (µg/cm²)

Applications of the sebumeter

🧑‍🔬

Skin typing

Classifies skin as oily, normal, dry or combination (T-zone vs cheeks).
🧴

Oil-control claims

Mattifying creams, oil-free moisturisers, blotting powders, pore-refining products.
🔴

Anti-acne products

Show reduced sebum after salicylic acid or zinc products.
🧼

Cleansers and shampoos

How much oil a face wash or shampoo removes, and how fast it comes back (sebum excretion rate).
💆‍♀️

Scalp and hair

Oily-scalp shampoos and anti-dandruff claims, since sebum feeds Malassezia.
📚

Research

Effect of age, hormones, season, diet or drugs (e.g. isotretinoin) on sebum output.

✅ Advantages

  • Non-invasive, painless and quick
  • Objective and reproducible thanks to constant spring pressure and zero calibration
  • Measures lipids only, so sweat and water do not interfere
  • Small probe usable on any site, including the scalp
  • Hygienic cassette, with fresh tape for every reading
VS

⚠️ Limitations

  • Measures only surface lipid: sebum plus epidermal lipids plus any product left on the skin
  • Affected by recent washing, cosmetics, skin temperature and time of day
  • Tape can saturate on very oily skin, and the area sampled is small
  • Gives the amount but not the composition of sebum
  • Hair on the site and uneven pressure cause errors

💊 Pharmacy example

A company launching a 2 % salicylic acid "oil-control" face wash measures forehead sebum with the Sebumeter at baseline, and at 1 h and 4 weeks, against an untreated side. A significant drop in µg/cm² supports the claim "controls oil for hours".

⚠️ Common mistake

Mixing the units up. Sebumeter = µg/cm² (a real amount). Corneometer = arbitrary units (a relative value). Tewameter = g/h/m² (a rate).

3 Corneometer: Measuring the Water

How well hydrated is the top layer of the skin? The corneometer "feels" water electrically.

🎬 Story mode

The Fairy Godmother has a glass-slipper-smooth wand tip. When she touches it to a cheek, a tiny invisible electric field dips into the very top layer of the skin. Water is a "superstar" in an electric field: it stores charge about 10 times better than the other things in skin. So a juicy, hydrated cheek makes the wand's meter swing high, and a dry, flaky cheek leaves it low. No current flows into Cinderella, because the glass keeps the metal from touching her.

📘 Exam definition

The Corneometer is a non-invasive instrument that measures the hydration of the stratum corneum by capacitance measurement. The stratum corneum acts as the dielectric of a measuring capacitor. Because water has a much higher dielectric constant (≈ 81) than the other skin components (mostly below 7), any change in skin water content changes the capacitance. This is displayed in arbitrary Corneometer units (0–120 AU).

Construction and principle, point by point

  • Probe head: two sets of gold tracks laid side by side (an interdigitated capacitor). An alternating electric field forms between neighbouring + and − tracks.
  • A thin glass lamina covers the tracks, so there is no galvanic (metal-to-skin) contact. No current flows through the skin, there is no polarisation, and salts on the skin have little influence.
  • A spring presses the head on the skin with constant pressure.
  • The field penetrates only the uppermost part of the stratum corneum (the manufacturer states about 10–20 µm), so the instrument reads surface hydration and not deeper water or blood.
  • Capacitance C = ε₀εᵣA/d. With A and d fixed by the probe, C depends only on εᵣ (dielectric constant), which depends mainly on water.
  • A reading takes about 1 second, which is too short for the probe itself to occlude the skin and raise its water. Results are in arbitrary units, 0–120.

Procedure

🧘Acclimatise 15–30 mincontrolled room
📍Mark the test sitee.g. volar forearm
📏Baseline readings3–5 per site, take the mean
🧴Apply moisturiserplus an untreated control
⏱️Re-measuree.g. 1 h, 4 h, 8 h, 24 h
📈% change vs control

Applications

🧴

Moisturiser efficacy

Short-term (hours) and long-term (weeks) hydration claims.
🏜️

Dry-skin studies

Xerosis, winter dryness, ageing skin, atopic skin.
🧼

Cleansers and soaps

Shows how much a soap dries the skin compared with a syndet bar.
🛡️

Occlusives and barrier creams

Development of products that hold water in.
🔥

Together with TEWL and erythema readings.
🧑‍🔬

Dry vs normal skin, and differences between body sites.

✅ Advantages

  • Fast (1 s), non-invasive and painless
  • No galvanic contact, so salts and product residues have minimal influence
  • Reproducible thanks to constant spring pressure
  • Small, light probe usable on any body site
  • The most widely used hydration method, so results can be compared with published studies
VS

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Arbitrary units: a relative value, not the absolute % water content
  • Reads only the superficial stratum corneum
  • Affected by room temperature, humidity, sweating and skin temperature
  • Probe angle, pressure and hairy or uneven skin cause errors; holding the probe too long occludes the skin
  • Freshly applied wet products can give falsely high values, so a waiting time is needed

4 TEWL: Is Elsa's Ice Wall Holding?

Transepidermal water loss is the single best number for how well the skin barrier is working.

🎬 Story mode

Elsa builds an ice palace: blocks of ice (corneocytes) stuck together with frosty "mortar" (intercellular lipids: ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids). Inside, the palace is warm and full of water (the living epidermis). A strong wall lets only a tiny trickle of water vapour seep out. If harsh soap, sunburn or eczema cracks the wall, water rushes out. Measuring that escaping vapour tells us how strong Elsa's wall is. High TEWL means a leaky wall.

📘 Exam definition

Transepidermal water loss (TEWL) is the amount of water that passively diffuses from the deeper, water-rich layers of the skin through the stratum corneum and evaporates from the skin surface, per unit area per unit time. It does not include sweat. It is expressed in g/h/m² (g m⁻² h⁻¹) and is the main index of skin barrier function. A higher TEWL means a more damaged barrier.

Principle: Fick's law of diffusion

Water vapour moves from where there is more (just above the skin) to where there is less (room air). Close to the skin surface a thin layer of air forms in which water-vapour pressure falls steadily with distance. The steeper this gradient, the more water is escaping. Fick's first law links the two:

dm/dt = − D · A · (dp/dx) dm/dt = mass of water transported per unit time (TEWL) · D = diffusion constant of water vapour in air · A = skin area · dp/dx = vapour-pressure gradient (change in vapour pressure p with distance x from the skin)

Each vapour pressure is calculated from relative humidity × saturated vapour pressure at that temperature. That is why every TEWL probe needs humidity and temperature sensors.

Method 1: Open-chamber method ⭐ (most widely used)

Instruments: Tewameter® (TM 300 and newer TM Hex), Evaporimeter (the original open-chamber instrument), DermaLab.

  • The probe is a small hollow cylinder (about 10 mm across and 20 mm high) that is open at both ends. It is held gently and perpendicular on the skin.
  • Two pairs of sensors (each a thermistor for temperature plus a hygrosensor for relative humidity) sit at two fixed heights above the skin. Newer probes carry many more pairs.
  • From T and RH the instrument calculates the vapour pressure at each height (p₁, p₂). The difference divided by the distance gives dp/dx, and Fick's law converts it into TEWL in g/h/m².
  • The probe head is often heated to near skin temperature so the reading settles faster. A reading takes about 20–30 s, once the value has stabilised.

✅ Advantages

  • Does not disturb the skin's microclimate (no occlusion)
  • Continuous measurement, with no waiting time between readings
  • Very sensitive at low (normal) TEWL values
  • The most validated method, with established standardisation guidelines
VS

⚠️ Disadvantages

  • Sensitive to air currents, room humidity and temperature, so it needs a draft-free, controlled room
  • The probe must be kept still and perpendicular
  • Less reliable at very high TEWL (wounds)
  • Difficult to use on curved sites or in awkward positions

Method 2: Closed-chamber (unventilated) method

Instrument: VapoMeter (a closed, unventilated chamber). The top of the cylinder is closed. Water vapour from the skin builds up inside, and a humidity sensor records how fast the relative humidity rises in the first few seconds. That rate of increase is converted to TEWL.

  • ✅ Not affected by room air currents; battery-operated, portable, and usable in any position (face, scalp, even upside-down).
  • ⚠️ Not continuous: humidity collects inside, so the probe must be lifted and dried between readings. The closed cup occludes the skin if held too long, and it is less sensitive at low TEWL values.
  • Condenser-chamber variant (AquaFlux): a closed chamber whose lid is a condenser cooled below 0 °C. It traps vapour as ice, so readings can be continuous and are independent of room air.

Method 3: Ventilated-chamber (gas-flow) method

A chamber is placed on the skin and a carrier gas (dry air or nitrogen) of known humidity flows through it at a constant rate. The gas picks up water evaporating from the skin, and a hygrometer (or electrolytic water analyser) measures the humidity of the outgoing gas.

TEWL = flow rate × (humidityout − humidityin) ÷ skin area
  • ✅ Continuous and independent of room air movement; good for laboratory and research set-ups.
  • ⚠️ The dry gas artificially increases evaporation and dries the skin surface, so TEWL tends to be over-estimated. The apparatus is bulky, needs flow calibration, and is not portable.

Applications of TEWL measurement

🛡️

Barrier-repair claims

Ceramide creams and moisturisers that bring a raised TEWL back to normal.
🧴

Occlusive efficacy

Petrolatum and other occlusive films reduce TEWL immediately.
🧼

Irritancy of surfactants

Soaps and SLS damage the barrier and raise TEWL, so mildness can be ranked.
🩹

Dermatology

Atopic dermatitis, psoriasis, wound healing, newborn skin.
💊

Transdermal / topical drugs

Effect of penetration enhancers on the barrier, and integrity of skin samples.
☀️

Photo-damage

Barrier recovery after UV exposure or sunburn.
💊 Pharmacy example

When a pharmaceutics lab tests whether a new penetration enhancer (say, oleic acid in a gel) disrupts the skin, they measure TEWL before and after application. A big rise means the enhancer is loosening the lipid "mortar". Good for drug penetration, but a warning for skin irritation.

⭐ Exam tip

"Explain TEWL. How is it measured?" (5 marks) = definition + units + normal range (1m) → Fick's law formula (1m) → open chamber with diagram (1.5m) → closed and ventilated chambers (1m) → factors / applications (0.5m). The 2-mark version: definition + units + "measured by open-chamber Tewameter (vapour-pressure gradient, Fick's law), closed-chamber VapoMeter, or ventilated chamber".

⚠️ Common mistake

TEWL is not sweating. It is passive, invisible water loss through the barrier. Sweat is active glandular secretion and is a source of error in TEWL measurement. Also remember: high TEWL = bad barrier (students often write the reverse).

5 Skin Colour: The Magic Mirror

"Magic Mirror on the wall…" Unlike the Queen's mirror, our instruments don't judge who is fairest. They measure melanin, redness and lightness as numbers.

🎬 Story mode

The Evil Queen's Magic Mirror only gave opinions. The Fairy Godmother's lab mirror is better: it shines coloured lights on the cheek and listens to what bounces back. Brown melanin swallows red and infrared light; red haemoglobin swallows green light. From what is missing in the reflection, the mirror reports two honest numbers, a melanin index and an erythema index, and it can also describe the colour as three coordinates: L*, a*, b*.

📘 Exam definition

Skin colour results from light absorbed and scattered by the skin's chromophores: melanin (eumelanin brown-black, pheomelanin red-yellow, in the epidermis), haemoglobin (oxy- and deoxy-, in dermal blood vessels), carotenoids (yellow) and bilirubin. It is measured non-invasively by reflectance methods: narrow-band reflectance spectrophotometry (Mexameter), tristimulus colorimetry (Chromameter, CIE L*a*b*) and diffuse reflectance spectroscopy, supported by visual scoring and imaging.

Why measure skin colour in cosmetics?

🌸

Skin-lightening / anti-spot

Fall in melanin index or rise in L* with niacinamide, kojic acid, vitamin C.
☀️

Sunscreens and tanning

Erythema after UV, tanning response, self-tanners.
🔥

Irritation / redness

Erythema index for patch tests and anti-redness claims.
💄

Make-up shade matching

Foundation shades, and the even-tone effect of make-up.

A. Narrow-band reflectance: Mexameter® MX 18

  • The probe emits light of three defined wavelengths: green 568 nm, red 660 nm and near-infrared 880 nm.
  • A receiver measures only the light diffusely reflected by the skin. Because the emitted amount is known, the amount absorbed can be calculated.
  • Melanin index uses 660 + 880 nm, chosen for their different absorption by pigment. Erythema index uses 568 nm (haemoglobin's absorption peak) + 660 nm, which avoids interference from other colours such as bilirubin.
  • Results come in about 1 s as indices from 0 to 999. A spring gives constant pressure. Other narrow-band instruments include the DermaSpectrometer.

B. Tristimulus colorimetry: Chromameter and the CIE L*a*b* system

A Chromameter (e.g. Minolta CR-series) flashes standard white light (a xenon lamp) on the skin. Three photodetectors with filters matching the human eye's response (the CIE standard observer) record the reflected light. The instrument expresses colour as three coordinates in the CIE 1976 L*a*b* colour space:

CoordinateMeaningSkin relevance
L*Lightness: 0 = black, 100 = whiteFalls with tanning and pigmentation; rises with lightening products
a*+ = red, − = greenRises with erythema, irritation, sunburn
b*+ = yellow, − = blueRises with melanin and tanning, and with carotene
ITA° = [arctan((L* − 50) / b*)] × 180/π Individual Typology Angle: higher ITA° = lighter skin; used to group skin as very light, light, intermediate, tan, brown or dark (cut-offs vary between studies)
ΔE* = √(ΔL*² + Δa*² + Δb*²) total colour difference between two readings, e.g. before vs after a product

➕ Bonus: how skin's pigment responds to UV
  • Immediate pigment darkening (IPD): within minutes of UVA, fades within hours (existing melanin is oxidised and redistributed).
  • Persistent pigment darkening (PPD): lasts from hours to about a day (used in UVA-PF testing of sunscreens).
  • Delayed tanning: appears after about 2–3 days as melanocytes make new melanin.
  • Alongside these comes erythema (sunburn redness, mainly UVB), which the a* value and the erythema index track.

6 Hair Tensile Strength: Rapunzel's Rope

"Rapunzel, Rapunzel, let down your hair!" How much pulling can one hair take before it snaps? And how do bleach, perms and conditioners change that?

🎬 Story mode

In Tangled, Rapunzel's hair becomes a rope, a swing, a lasso, even a bridge. Imagine Flynn testing one strand. At first it stretches a little and springs back (elastic, Hookean region). Pull harder and it suddenly stretches a lot with little extra force, as the coiled keratin springs unwind (yield region). Near the end it stiffens again until… snap! (post-yield and break). If Mother Gothel had bleached that hair, it would snap much sooner. That is exactly what a tensile tester shows.

📘 Exam definition

Tensile strength of hair is the maximum stress (force per unit cross-sectional area) that a single hair fibre can withstand when it is stretched along its length, just before it breaks. It is measured with a tensile tester (extensometer) that elongates the fibre at a constant rate and records load against extension, giving the stress–strain curve.

Quick recap: why hair is strong

A hair shaft has three parts: the cuticle (overlapping scales outside), the cortex (the bulk, made of α-keratin macrofibrils packed in a sulphur-rich matrix and held by disulphide (S–S), hydrogen and salt bonds) and the medulla (the core, sometimes absent). The cortex carries almost all of the tensile load, so tensile testing mainly reports the health of the cortex.

Formulae to write

Stress (σ) = F / A force ÷ cross-sectional area (N/m² = Pa; usually MPa)
Strain (ε) = ΔL / L₀ × 100 extension ÷ original (gauge) length, in %
Young's modulus (E) = σ / ε slope of the initial straight (Hookean) part = stiffness
Work to break = area under the curve toughness: energy needed to break the fibre

Instruments: universal testing machines such as the Instron, and dedicated single-fibre systems such as the Dia-Stron Miniature Tensile Tester (with automatic laser diameter measurement and an autosampler). Some labs also use cyclic fatigue testing (repeated small loads until the fibre breaks), which is closer to real-life grooming damage.

Factors that affect hair tensile properties

💧

Water / humidity

Wet hair has a lower modulus and lower yield stress, and stretches more. Water breaks hydrogen bonds. Always state the RH.
🧪

Chemical treatment

Bleaching (oxidises S–S to cysteic acid), perming, straightening (reduce S–S) and oxidative dyes all lower break stress.
☀️

Weathering

UV, chlorine and sea water degrade keratin, especially at the tips.
🔥

Heat and grooming

Flat irons, blow-drying, harsh combing and brushing.
📏

Fibre geometry

Diameter, elliptical cross-section, ethnic hair type, root vs tip.
⚙️

Test conditions

Strain rate (hair is viscoelastic), gauge length, temperature, clamping.

⭐ Limitations of hair tensile strength measurement (asked Nov 2022)

  1. Cross-sectional area is uncertain. Hair is elliptical and its diameter varies along the length, so stress (F/A) carries error unless the diameter is measured accurately for every fibre.
  2. High fibre-to-fibre variability. Results vary widely between hairs, people and head regions, so many fibres (often 25–50 or more) and statistics are needed.
  3. Strongly affected by humidity and temperature. Results are meaningless unless the conditions are strictly controlled and reported.
  4. Mainly reflects the cortex. Damage or conditioning effects limited to the cuticle (surface) are poorly detected, and conditioners often show little change.
  5. A single-fibre, single-pull test does not reflect real-life breakage, which comes from repeated low-force combing and grooming (fatigue) acting on a whole bundle of hair.
  6. Clamping problems. Slippage, or breaks at the clamp jaws, give invalid results.
  7. It is destructive (the fibre is broken), time-consuming, and needs expensive equipment and trained operators.
  8. Results depend on strain rate and gauge length (viscoelastic behaviour), so values from different labs are hard to compare.

Applications

  • Quantify damage from bleaches, perms, relaxers, oxidative dyes, heat styling and UV.
  • Prove strengthening and repair claims (protein hydrolysates, bond-building treatments, conditioners).
  • Compare formulations during development; quality control of hair products and of wigs and extensions.
  • The "20 % index": the ratio of work needed to extend a wet fibre by 20 % after vs before a treatment, used as a damage index within the yield region.

7 Hair Combing Properties (Combability)

This line is in the PCI syllabus for Unit IV and turns up often in question banks. A short, sweet topic.

🎬 Story mode

After her escape, Rapunzel's tangled hair needs a brush. Without conditioner, the comb snags and pulls hard. After a lovely conditioner, it glides. A lab can measure that pull: fit a comb to the tensile tester and draw it through a hair tress. The force recorded is the combing force.

📘 Exam definition

Combability is the ease with which hair can be combed. It is measured as the force (and work) needed to draw a comb through a standard hair tress, wet or dry. Lower combing force and work mean better combability (better conditioning, less damage).

Procedure (instrumental combing test)

💧 Wet combing

Detangling after shampoo. Wet hair swells, its cuticle lifts and friction is high. Conditioners (cationic surfactants such as behentrimonium chloride, polyquaterniums, silicones such as dimethicone) cut wet combing work sharply.

🌬️ Dry combing

Snagging and static flyaway in dry hair. Cationic conditioners neutralise the negative charge on the hair and silicones lubricate.

Also measured: number of broken fibres collected after repeated combing (breakage), and sensory panel scores (half-head tests). Benefits of combing: detangles and styles, removes loose hairs, dirt and flakes, spreads sebum along the shaft for shine, and gently massages the scalp.

8 Soaps & Syndet Bars: Evolution and Skin Benefits

Q. Describe sebumeter and corneometer with a neat labelled diagram.
Essay · 10 marks · KUHS Dec 2023 · ~25 min · ~3 pages
Intro: non-invasive skin bioengineering (1m) → Sebumeter: definition, principle, labelled diagram, procedure, applications, merits and limits (4m) → Corneometer: same pattern (4m) → Comparison table + conclusion (1m)

Introduction

Cosmetic efficacy claims such as "oil control" or "moisturising" must be substantiated objectively. Skin bioengineering instruments measure skin properties non-invasively. The Sebumeter measures surface sebum (oil) and the Corneometer measures stratum corneum hydration (water). Both are made by Courage + Khazaka (Germany) and are used worldwide in cosmetic evaluation.

A. SEBUMETER

Definition: An instrument that measures the casual sebum level on the skin, scalp or hair surface by grease-spot photometry, giving results in µg sebum/cm².

Principle (grease-spot photometry): A special matt (opaque) plastic tape, ~0.1 mm thick, becomes transparent when it absorbs sebum, just as an oil spot makes paper translucent. The more sebum, the more transparent the tape, and the more light is transmitted through it. A photocell measures the transmitted light, and a microprocessor converts it to µg/cm².

Diagram: draw Fig 4.1: (a) measuring cassette with tape reels, spring, 64 mm² tape window and trigger, pressed on skin; (b) main unit with aperture, lamp → tape → photocell, and display.

Construction: ① measuring cassette with a roll of matt tape; ② a window exposing 64 mm² of tape; ③ a spring giving constant pressure; ④ a trigger to advance fresh tape; ⑤ the main unit with light source, photocell, microprocessor and display.

Procedure: acclimatise the volunteer (no products or washing on the site) → zero-calibrate on unused tape → press the cassette on the skin for a fixed time (~30 s) → insert into the device aperture → read µg/cm² → advance the tape for the next reading.

Applications: classification of skin type (oily, dry, combination); efficacy of oil-control, mattifying and anti-acne products; sebum removal by cleansers and shampoos and the rate of re-oiling; oily scalp and anti-dandruff claims; research on the effects of age, hormones and drugs.

Merits: rapid, non-invasive, reproducible, insensitive to water and sweat, hygienic. Limitations: measures only surface lipids (including epidermal lipids and product residues); affected by washing, temperature and time of day; tape saturates on very oily skin; gives no information on composition.

B. CORNEOMETER

Definition: An instrument that measures the hydration of the stratum corneum by capacitance, giving results in arbitrary units (0–120).

Principle (capacitance): The probe is a capacitor. The stratum corneum acts as the dielectric. Water has a dielectric constant of about 81, far higher than other skin constituents (mostly below 7). So the more water in the stratum corneum, the higher the capacitance (C = ε₀εᵣA/d), which the instrument converts to Corneometer units.

Diagram: draw Fig 4.2: probe handle → spring → probe head with interdigitated gold tracks (+/−) covered by a glass lamina → skin layers (SC measured to ~10–20 µm) with curved field lines.

Construction: ① gold conductor tracks forming the capacitor plates; ② a thin glass lamina so there is no galvanic contact (no current through the skin, no polarisation); ③ a spring giving constant pressure; ④ electronics and display.

Procedure: acclimatise 15–30 min in a controlled room → baseline readings (3–5, mean) on a marked site → apply product (plus an untreated control) → measure at time points (each reading ~1 s) → calculate % change.

Applications: moisturiser efficacy (short and long term); dry-skin and ageing studies; drying effect of soaps and cleansers; development of occlusive and barrier creams; irritation studies; skin typing.

Merits: fast (1 s, no occlusion), non-invasive, minimal influence of salts and residues, reproducible. Limitations: arbitrary (relative) units; superficial layer only; affected by temperature, humidity, sweating, probe pressure and hair.

Q. Measurement of trans-epidermal water loss in cosmetics. / Explain TEWL. How is it measured?
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS May 2022 & Dec 2023 · ~12 min
Definition + significance (1m) → Fick's law principle (1m) → Open-chamber method with diagram (1½m) → Closed and ventilated chambers (1m) → Factors / applications (½m)

Definition: TEWL is the passive diffusion of water from the inner skin through the stratum corneum to the surface and its evaporation, excluding sweat. Unit: g/h/m². It is the main index of skin barrier integrity: high TEWL = damaged barrier (dry, irritated skin). Healthy skin is roughly 4–10 g/h/m² under ambient conditions.

Principle: Fick's law of diffusion, dm/dt = −D·A·dp/dx, where dm/dt = water transported per unit time, D = diffusion constant, A = area, dp/dx = vapour-pressure gradient above the skin. Vapour pressure is calculated from relative humidity and temperature.

1. Open-chamber method (Tewameter, Evaporimeter): a hollow cylinder (~10 mm × 20 mm) open at both ends is placed on the skin. Two pairs of sensors (temperature + RH) at two heights give p₁ and p₂, so the gradient dp/dx is converted to TEWL. ✓ continuous, no occlusion. ✗ disturbed by air currents. Diagram: Fig 4.4.

2. Closed-chamber method (VapoMeter): the cylinder is closed at the top. The rate of increase of humidity inside is recorded and converted to TEWL. ✓ unaffected by drafts, portable, any position. ✗ not continuous, occludes the skin. The condenser-chamber version (AquaFlux) traps vapour on a cooled condenser.

3. Ventilated-chamber method: dry carrier gas of known humidity flows over the skin and the humidity of the exiting gas is measured. TEWL = flow × ΔRH ÷ area. ✓ continuous. ✗ dry gas over-dries skin, bulky.

Factors to control: room temperature and RH, drafts, sweating (acclimatise 15–30 min), skin temperature, body site, probe pressure and angle, product residues.

Applications: barrier-repair and moisturiser claims, mildness of soaps and surfactants, irritation testing, atopic skin, penetration-enhancer studies.

Q. Explain the principle involved in the measurement of tensile strength of hair and transepidermal water loss.
Short note · 5 marks · KUHS Model paper · ~12 min
Part A Hair tensile (2½m: definition, formulae, tester sketch, curve) → Part B TEWL (2½m: definition, Fick's law, open-chamber sketch, other methods)

A. Tensile strength of hair

Definition: the maximum stress a single hair fibre can bear before breaking when stretched along its axis.

Principle: A fibre of known gauge length and cross-sectional area (from diameter by laser micrometer) is clamped in a tensile tester (Instron / Dia-Stron) and extended at a constant rate. A load cell records force against extension, which is converted to stress (σ = F/A) and strain (ε = ΔL/L₀). The stress–strain curve (Fig 4.9) has three regions: Hookean (≈0–2 %, linear and reversible, slope = Young's modulus), yield (≈2–30 %, α-helix → β-sheet, plateau) and post-yield (beyond ≈30 %, stiffening until break). Break stress, break strain, modulus and work to break are reported. Damage from bleaching or perming lowers break stress; tests are run at controlled humidity because water weakens hair.

B. Transepidermal water loss

Definition: passive water loss through the stratum corneum (g/h/m²), an index of barrier function.

Principle: Fick's law, dm/dt = −D·A·dp/dx. In the open-chamber Tewameter, two temperature + humidity sensor pairs at different heights in an open cylinder measure the vapour-pressure gradient above the skin, which is converted to TEWL (Fig 4.4). The closed-chamber method measures the rate of humidity rise in a sealed cup, and the ventilated-chamber method measures the humidity picked up by a carrier gas. Standard conditions (acclimatisation, controlled temperature and RH, no drafts) are essential.

Q. Write the limitations of hair tensile strength measurement.
Brief · 2 marks · KUHS Nov 2022 · 4–6 bullets
  • Cross-sectional area is hard to measure accurately (elliptical, variable diameter), so stress values carry error.
  • Large variation between fibres, so many fibres and statistics are needed.
  • Highly dependent on humidity, temperature, strain rate and gauge length.
  • Reflects mainly cortex strength; cuticle damage and conditioning are poorly detected.

Biggest error source in open-chamber TEWL?
Air currents and drafts (plus sweating, room temperature and RH).
Mexameter wavelengths?
Green 568, red 660, NIR 880 nm. MI = 660 + 880; EI = 568 + 660.
L*, a*, b* mean?
L* lightness (0 black–100 white); a* red(+)/green(−); b* yellow(+)/blue(−).
3 regions of the hair stress–strain curve?
Hookean (≈0–2 %), yield (≈2–30 %, α→β), post-yield (beyond ≈30 % → break).
Effect of water on hair tensile properties?
Lower modulus and yield stress, more extension. Water breaks H-bonds.
Combability is measured by…?
Combing force/work as a comb is drawn through a tress on a tensile tester.
Soap vs syndet pH?
Soap ~9–10 (alkaline); syndet ~5.5–7 (skin-friendly).

🎯 1-Minute Quiz

The Sebumeter tape works because sebum makes it…
Grease-spot photometry: more sebum → more transparent tape → more light reaches the photocell.
The Corneometer measures hydration using…
Water's high dielectric constant (≈81) raises the capacitance of the probe.
A HIGH TEWL value means…
More water escaping through the stratum corneum = leaky barrier (Elsa's wall is cracked).
Which TEWL method is most disturbed by air currents?
The open cylinder is exposed to room air, so drafts disturb the vapour gradient.
The erythema index of the Mexameter mainly reflects…
Green light (568 nm) is absorbed by haemoglobin, so redness is read as EI.
In the hair stress–strain curve, the α-helix → β-sheet change happens in the…
The yield plateau (≈2–30 %) is where the keratin coils unfold with little extra stress.
Which is a limitation of hair tensile testing?
The cortex carries the load; also, many fibres are needed and the test is destructive.
In CIE L*a*b*, a lightening product that works will mainly increase…
L* is lightness (0 black → 100 white).

📝 One-Page Cheat Sheet: Unit 4

Read this the night before the exam. Everything important, nothing extra.

🛢️ Sebumeter

Grease-spot photometry · matt tape (~0.1 mm) → transparent with sebum · photocell reads light transmission · µg/cm² · 64 mm² window · spring, trigger, cassette · ~30 s. Uses: skin type, oil control, acne, shampoos.

💧 Corneometer

Capacitance · SC = dielectric · water ε ≈ 81 (others below 7) · gold tracks + glass lamina (no galvanic contact) · spring · ~10–20 µm · 1 s · 0–120 AU. Uses: moisturisers, dry skin, cleansers.

🧊 TEWL

Passive water loss through the SC (not sweat) · g/h/m² · normal ≈ 4–10 · Fick: dm/dt = −D·A·dp/dx · Open (Tewameter, 2 T+RH pairs, Ø10×20 mm) · Closed (VapoMeter; condenser AquaFlux) · Ventilated (carrier gas). Control: room T/RH, drafts, sweat, site, probe pressure.

🪞 Skin colour

Chromophores: melanin, haemoglobin, carotene, bilirubin · Mexameter: 568/660/880 nm → MI and EI (0–999) · Chromameter: L*(light) a*(red) b*(yellow), ITA° · DRS spectrophotometer · Fitzpatrick, photography, Wood's lamp.

💇‍♀️ Hair tensile

σ = F/A, ε = ΔL/L₀, E = σ/ε · Instron / Dia-Stron · Hookean ≈0–2 % → Yield ≈2–30 % (α→β) → Post-yield → break · water, bleach, perm ↓ strength · Limits: area error, variability, RH, cortex-only, not fatigue, destructive.

🪮 Combing · 🧼 Syndets

Combability = combing force/work on a tensile tester (wet and dry); conditioners ↓ it. Soap pH 9–10 vs syndet 5.5–7 (SCI); syndets give lower TEWL, more hydration, less irritation, no scum.
